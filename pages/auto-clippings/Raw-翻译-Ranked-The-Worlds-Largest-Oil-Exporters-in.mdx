Which Countries Export the Most Oil?

Key Takeaways

The United States exports 10.7 million barrels of oil per day, leading Saudi Arabia by 2.8 million barrels per day.

The Middle East accounts for 29.2% of global oil exports, the largest share of any region.

Oil is one of the world’s most heavily traded commodities, and the countries that ship it abroad can influence fuel costs far beyond their borders.

How the U.S. Became the World’s Top Oil Exporter

The United States ranks first at 10.7 million barrels per day, ahead of Saudi Arabia at 7.9 million. Together, the two countries account for nearly a quarter of global oil exports.

The U.S. position at the top owes much to the shale boom. Horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing unlocked more oil from tight rock formations, while the lifting of most crude oil export restrictions in 2015 opened up overseas markets.

Russia ranks third at 6.6 million barrels per day, bringing the top three countries’ combined share to 33.0%. Canada follows at 4.1 million, ahead of the United Arab Emirates at 3.9 million.

Because these figures include petroleum products as well as crude oil, major refining and trading hubs can also rank highly. This helps explain the presence of countries such as the Netherlands and Singapore among the world’s larger exporters.

The Middle East Supplies 29% of Global Oil Exports

At the regional level, the picture shifts. The Middle East exports 22.4 million barrels per day, accounting for 29.2% of the global total.

Europe follows at 16.1 million barrels per day, or 21.0%, narrowly ahead of North America’s 15.7 million, or 20.5%. Asia-Pacific supplies another 10.9 million barrels per day, representing 14.2%.

The Middle East’s

vast oil reserves

help explain its lead. Ancient seas deposited organic-rich sediments, while reservoir rocks and sealing layers allowed oil to accumulate underground.

Saudi Arabia supplies more than a third of the region’s exports. Its relatively low-cost fields, extensive pipeline network, and investments in overseas refineries help turn those reserves into reliable access to customers. Iraq and Kuwait add 3.5 million and 2.4 million barrels per day, respectively.

Together, the Middle East and North America supply 49.7% of global exports. On the buying side, Asia-Pacific receives

50.1% of global oil imports

, highlighting the scale of oil trade between distant regions.
