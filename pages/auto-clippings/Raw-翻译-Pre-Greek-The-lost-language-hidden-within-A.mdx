Pre-Greek: The lost language hidden within Ancient Greek
前希腊语：隐藏在古希腊语中的失落语言

Around 1,000 Greek words can’t be traced to any Indo-European language. Where do they come from?
大约有1000个希腊语词汇无法追溯到任何印欧语系语言。它们来自哪里？

Despite centuries of effort, linguists have not been able to trace these words back to Proto-Indo-European (PIE), the parent language from which all the other Indo-European (IE) languages descend. Nor have they identified words in neighboring languages that Greek might have borrowed them from.
尽管经过几个世纪的努力，语言学家仍无法将这些词汇追溯到原始印欧语（PIE），即所有其他印欧语系（IE）语言的母语。他们也没能在邻近语言中找到希腊语可能借用这些词汇的来源。

Where, then, do these words come from? In this issue of the Linguistic Discovery newsletter, we’ll dig through layers of linguistic history to reveal the mystery language that influenced Ancient Greek.
那么，这些词究竟来自哪里？在本期《语言发现》通讯中，我们将挖掘层层语言历史，揭示影响古希腊语的神秘语言。

Paleo-European / Old European Languages
古欧语 / 旧欧语

All languages contain a fossil record of their history.
所有语言都包含着其历史的化石记录。

While Indo-European languages have been around for a long time, and Greek has likewise been spoken in Greece for several millennia, Indo-European languages haven’t always blanketed the majority of Europe like they do today. At one point the Indo-Europeans were pastoralist newcomers to a strange land, one that had been occupied by Neolithic farming societies for millennia.
虽然印欧语系语言已经存在了很长时间，希腊语在希腊也已经被说了几千年，但印欧语系语言并非一直像今天这样覆盖欧洲大部分地区。曾几何时，印欧人是这片陌生土地上的游牧新来者，而这片土地已经被新石器时代的农耕社会占据了数千年。

The peoples who preceded the Indo-Europeans—called Early European Farmers—spoke any number of Paleo-European (a.k.a. Old European) languages. Very few of these left any written evidence (just Aquitanian, Etruscan, Iberian, and a handful of others), and only one survives today (Basque). Even so, these Old European languages left an indelible mark on the Indo-European languages that succeeded them.
在印欧人之前的民族——被称为早期欧洲农人——说着多种古欧语（又称旧欧语）。这些语言中极少留下书面证据（仅有阿基坦语、伊特鲁里亚语、伊比利亚语及少数其他几种），而且今天只有一种存活下来（巴斯克语）。即便如此，这些旧欧语对后来取代它们的印欧语系语言留下了不可磨灭的印记。

Imagine you’re a band of early Hellenic (Greek) speakers whose ancestors had migrated over generations from the Pontic–Caspian Steppe into the eastern Mediterranean. Along the way, those ancestors encountered all sorts of novel foods, plants, animals, and terrain—things like olives, hyacinth, figs, and cypresses. Even the sea was unfamiliar at one point, migrating as your ancestors did from a landlocked steppe. What do you call all these things?
想象你是一群早期希腊语使用者，你的祖先经过数代人的迁徙，从庞蒂克-里海草原进入东地中海。沿途，这些祖先遇到了各种新奇的食物、植物、动物和地形——比如橄榄、风信子、无花果和柏树。甚至大海在某个时刻也是陌生的，因为你的祖先是从内陆草原迁徙而来的。你该如何称呼这一切？

One option is to recruit existing vocabulary for new purposes. For example, when the Greeks first described hippopotamuses in the mid-5th century BCE, they cleverly called them ‘river horses’ (ἵππος ὁ ποτάμιος [híppos ho potámios] ‘the horse of the river’ [lit. ‘the riverine horse’]; Herodotus, Histories 2.71, c. 440s BCE). This later gave rise to the compound ἱπποπόταμος (hippopótamos) ‘hippopotamus’ (Dioscorides, De materia medica 2.25, c. 50–70 CE). The Greeks even applied this strategy to the sea! One of the words the Ancient Greeks used for ‘sea’ was πόντος (póntos), which comes from PIE *póntoh₁s ‘path; road’. Every other branch of Indo-European kept the original ‘path/way’ meaning (e.g. Latin pōns ~ pontis ‘bridge’, from which English gets pontiff and pontificate), but Greek extended it to mean ‘sea’, apparently in the sense of ‘the water-way; the sea-crossing’. Another Ancient Greek word used poetically to refer to the ‘sea’ was ἅλς (háls), whose primary meaning was actually ‘salt’. The sea, then, was ‘the salt’.
一种选择是让现有词汇承担新的用途。例如，当希腊人在公元前5世纪中叶首次描述河马时，他们巧妙地将它们称为“河马”（ἵππος ὁ ποτάμιος [híppos ho potámios] “河的马” [字面意思“河边的马”]；希罗多德，《历史》2.71，约公元前440年代）。这后来产生了复合词 ἱπποπόταμος (hippopótamos) “河马”（迪奥斯科里德斯，《药物志》2.25，约公元50-70年）。希腊人甚至将这种策略应用于大海！古希腊人用来表示“海”的词之一是 πόντος (póntos)，它来自原始印欧语 *póntoh₁s “路径；道路”。印欧语系的其他每个分支都保留了最初的“路径/道路”含义（例如拉丁语 pōns ~ pontis “桥”，英语中的 pontiff 和 pontificate 即源于此），但希腊语将其引申为“海”，显然带有“水路；跨海航行”的意义。另一个古希腊语中用于诗意地指称“海”的词是 ἅλς (háls)，其主要含义实际上是“盐”。那么，海就是“盐”。

This strategy of coining new terms for novel experiences was, however, the minority one. Why invent new words for things the local Paleo-Europeans already had names for? Why not simply adopt the local name? That’s exactly what Indo-Europeans did as they settled across the continent, casually borrowing hundreds of words for plants, animals, types of terrain, places, and other natural phenomena. Thus it’s no surprise that the primary Greek word for ‘sea’ is actually θάλασσα (thálassa → English thalassocracy ‘rule of the sea’, used for maritime empires), which, unlike póntos and háls, has no firm Indo-European etymology. Linguists have long tried to connect this word to Indo-European, but no proposal has won wide acceptance. This is exactly what we’d expect: because the Proto-Indo-European language arose in the landlocked steppes, it had no need for—and has no securely reconstructable word for—‘sea’. (Linguists have, however, reconstructed terms for snow, cold, wolves, bears, and honey—all consistent with the temperate, continental interior.) Its daughter branches only acquired sea vocabulary later. As they reached the coasts, each language innovated or borrowed its own word for ‘sea’: English sea, Latin mar
然而，为新鲜事物创造新术语的做法只是少数情况。为什么要为当地古欧人已经有名称的事物发明新词呢？为什么不直接采用当地名称呢？这正是印欧人在这片大陆上定居下来时所做的事情，他们随意借用了数百个表示植物、动物、地形类型、地点和其他自然现象的词汇。因此，古希腊语中表示“海”的主要词实际上是 θάλασσα (thálassa → 英语 thalassocracy “海上统治”，用于指称海上帝国)，与 póntos 和 háls 不同，它没有可靠的印欧语词源。语言学家长期以来一直试图将这个单词与印欧语联系起来，但没有任何提议获得广泛接受。这正是我们所预期的：因为原始印欧语产生于内陆草原，它不需要——也没有一个可靠重构的——“海”这个词。（然而，语言学家已经重构了表示雪、寒冷、狼、熊和蜂蜜的词汇——这些都与温带大陆性内陆地区一致。）它的子语言分支只是后来才获得了海洋词汇。当它们到达海岸时，每种语言都自己创新或借用了表示“海”的词汇：英语 sea，拉丁语 mar
