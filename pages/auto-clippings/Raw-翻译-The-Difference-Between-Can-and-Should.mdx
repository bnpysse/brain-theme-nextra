Two months post-book launch, it’s time to start blogging again. Does anyone blog these days? Does anyone read blogs these days? I don’t know. The

“death of reading”

is certainly a popular hypothesis in 2026, with fried attention spans, TikTok, screen addiction, and the like, but that’s a topic for another blog post. I still find writing to be the best way to distill jumbled thoughts into concise ideas, so it’s time to resume that practice.

新书发布两个月后，是时候重新开始写博客了。如今还有人写博客吗？如今还有人读博客吗？我不知道。“阅读之死”在 2026 年无疑是一个流行的假说——注意力被炸毁、TikTok、屏幕成瘾等等——但那是另一篇博文的主题。我仍然觉得写作是将杂乱思绪提炼成简洁想法的最佳方式，所以是时候恢复这个习惯了。

This is a blog post about “AI.”

Of course, every blog post written by someone at least adjacent to the tech sector is about “AI,” so that’s not particularly interesting or novel. So this isn’t really a blog post about “AI.”

这是一篇关于“AI”的博文。

当然，任何由至少与科技行业沾边的人写的博文都是关于“AI”的，所以这并不特别有趣或新颖。所以这其实不是一篇关于“AI”的博文。

This is a blog post about indecision and impulsiveness and adaptation and the fact that, perhaps, friction and scarcity and inertia were actually net positive variables in the human lived experience.

这是一篇关于犹豫不决、冲动、适应性的博文，以及这样一个事实：也许，摩擦、稀缺和惯性实际上是人类生活体验中的净正向变量。

This is not a blog post about “burnout,” because “burnout” is a symptom of hammering away at a single thing for an uncommon length of time. This is a blog post about the opposite: the AI-enabled compulsion to simultaneously hammer away at a million things for an uncommonly short time.

这不是一篇关于“倦怠”的博文，因为“倦怠”是长时间专注于单一事物而产生的症状。这篇博文讲的是相反的情况：在 AI 的助力下，人们被迫在异常短的时间内同时处理无数件事情。

There’s a study in Barry Schwartz’s

The Paradox of Choice

that showed when a supermarket displayed 24 varieties of gourmet jam, 60% of shoppers stopped, but only 3% bought any. When they displayed only 6 varieties, fewer people stopped (40%), but 30% made a purchase.

巴里·施瓦茨（Barry Schwartz）的《选择的悖论》（The Paradox of Choice）中有一项研究显示，当超市展示 24 种精品果酱时，60% 的购物者会停下来，但只有 3% 的人购买。而当只展示 6 种时，停下来的人更少（40%），但 30% 的人购买了。

What’s the lesson here? We suck at making decisions, particularly when there are several “good” choices.

这里的教训是什么？我们不擅长做决定，尤其是当有多个“不错”的选择时。

Take dating apps as an example. Throw the entire meat market of eligible singles on the other side of a screen and

no shit

fertility rates are going sub-zero: as your number of perceived options approaches infinity, the “opportunity cost” of any particular option feels uncharacteristically high, which lowers the likelihood of committing to any one option, leading to a perpetual loop of infatuations that subsist until they are replaced by yet another infatuation, typically around the time that any real “friction” develops with your current infatuation.

以约会应用为例。把整个婚恋市场的合格单身人士都扔到屏幕另一端，然后——废话——生育率当然会跌到零以下：当你感知到的选项数量趋近于无穷时，任何特定选项的“机会成本”都会感觉异常之高，这降低了你对任何一个选项做出承诺的可能性，导致你陷入一轮又一轮的迷恋，直到被新的迷恋所取代——通常是在你与当前迷恋对象之间出现任何真正的“摩擦”时。

60% of shoppers stopped

(which, in our dating app instance, could mean anything from grabbed drinks to made out to had sex)

but only 3% bought any

(assumed any risk or vulnerability)

.

60% 的购物者停下来（在我们的约会应用例子中，这可能意味着从喝杯酒到接吻再到发生性关系），但只有 3% 的人购买（承担任何风险或暴露脆弱性）。

But this isn’t a blog post about sex, love, and situationships. This is a blog post about artificial intelligence, coding assistants, Claude, Grok Bot, Codex, tokens, harnesses, and “giving root access” to all of the above. Which is far more tantalizing than first dates, martinis, and the prospect of sex, right?

但这不是一篇关于性、爱情和暧昧关系的博文。这是一篇关于人工智能、编程助手、Claude、Grok Bot、Codex、token、工具链，以及给上述所有东西“授予 root 权限”的博文。这可比初次约会、马提尼酒和性爱的前景诱人多了，对吧？

A Codex subscription is just Raya for folks who know what “reinforcement learning is” in that, instead of having your retinas bombarded with cleavage and witty prompts, your brain turns to the unlimited possibilities of what you “could” build.

对于知道“强化学习是什么”的人来说，Codex 订阅就像 Raya（高端约会应用）——只不过不是让你的视网膜被事业线和俏皮话轰炸，而是让你的大脑转向你“可能”构建的无限可能性。

“Programming” used to suck, because so much of “programming” was simply refactoring, debugging, and, really, just trying to understand what the hell you were reading in a particular Github repo. You couldn’t even “code” at the speed of typing

(which, really, isn’t that fast)

, you could only “code” at the speed with which you could “comprehend” that which you were typing. Comprehension requires foundational knowledge, intuition refined through experience, and focus. Only so much (quality!) code could be written in so much time before you would have to take a break and resume your pursuit the following day.

“编程”过去很糟糕，因为“编程”的很大一部分只是重构、调试，以及——说实话——试图理解你在某个 GitHub 仓库里读到的到底是什么鬼东西。你甚至不能以打字的速度（其实打字也没多快）“写代码”，你只能以“理解”所写内容的速度来“写代码”。理解需要基础知识、通过经验磨练出的直觉，以及专注力。在不得不休息、第二天再继续之前，你只能在有限时间内写出这么多（高质量的！）代码。

This also meant that, obviously, there was a certain amount of “boot up” time required to context switch into a particular task. I have felt this with writing. When I was working on my book

(if you’ve made it this far,

buy your copy here

)

, something I quickly became aware of was that I probably needed, at a minimum, ~3 hours of uninterrupted time to write or it wasn’t worth getting started. It would take me ~30 minutes to fully context switch and get back in the flow of where I left off, and maybe another ~30 minutes to “wind down” whatever section I was working through before I could actually step away.

这也意味着，显然，要切换到某个特定任务需要一定的“启动”时间。我在写作时深有体会。当我写书时（如果你已经读到这里，点这里买一本），我很快意识到，我至少需要大约 3 小时不被打断的时间来写作，否则就不值得开始。我需要大约 30 分钟来完全切换上下文，回到上次停下的地方，可能还需要 30 分钟来“收尾”我正在写的部分，然后才能真正离开。

I can’t tell you how often I labored over a particular word, or sentence, or figure of speech used within a monologue, just to delete, then rewrite, the whole sentence or paragraph or page or chapter before the puzzle finally fit together. You need big chunks of time where you can’t be bothered by calls or texts or emails to work through that puzzle.

我无法告诉你，我多少次为一个词、一个句子或独白中的某个修辞手法绞尽脑汁，然后删掉、重写整个句子、段落、页面甚至章节，直到拼图终于拼合。你需要大块的时间，不被电话、短信或邮件打扰，才能解开那个谜题。

For me, this meant that 95% of my writing happened between the time I got off work on Friday and went to bed on Sunday. I had more cognitive energy and focus during those windows, and I could finally sit with the material long enough to make it coherent.

对我来说，这意味着我 95% 的写作都发生在周五下班后到周日睡觉前这段时间。在那段时间里，我有更多的认知能量和专注力，终于可以足够长时间地坐在材料前，把它整理得连贯起来。
