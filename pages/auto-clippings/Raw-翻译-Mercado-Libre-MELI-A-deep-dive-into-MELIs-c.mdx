【帖子标题】：Mercado Libre (MELI): A deep dive into MELI’s credit business

【帖子正文】：

Mercado Libre's credit business is talked about a lot. So we did some research and analysis. We looked at its credit portfolio composition (across product and geography), loan duration, exposure per user, funding model etc. We also benchmarked MELI's credit risk and returns, against Nubank across key metrics such as net-interest income margin after losses. Attached post has all the charts and good stuff

Key takeaways from the analysis:

MELI’s unsecured credit portfolio has significant exposure to higher-risk borrowers. MELI’s &gt;90-day past-due loans have remained between 17–19% of its gross loan portfolio since Q4 2023, versus 6–7% at NuBank. Its credit portfolio is highly sensitive to macroeconomic conditions. During the period of elevated inflation in 2022–23, MELI’s &gt;90-day past-due ratio increased sharply, reaching 30% in Q4 2022 and 28% in Q1 2023

That higher risk has been compensated by higher pricing, resulting in attractive returns in recent years. MELI generated a 59% annualized credit revenue yield in Q2 26, versus 38% for NuBank

MELI’s loan durations are very short (~3 months on average). This is an advantage, as it gives MELI greater flexibility to adjust credit availability and pricing as conditions change

However, questions remain about the sustainability of returns. MELI’s credit business has not been proven over time across different economic cycles. In adverse economic conditions, MELI could see a material uptick in credit losses, while loan originations could slow

MELI’s recent margin pressure is not a major concern to us. A material portion is driven by provision expense, which is an accounting measure that can exceed realized losses and non-performing loans. This is amplified by MELI’s focus on credit card expansion, where provisions are incurred upfront, while interest income builds up slowly as customers mature and begin carrying balances. As the credit card business matures, interest income should build and support margins
