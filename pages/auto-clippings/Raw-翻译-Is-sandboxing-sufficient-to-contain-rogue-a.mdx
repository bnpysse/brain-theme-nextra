【文章标题】：Is sandboxing sufficient to contain rogue agents?

沙盒机制能否有效约束失控的AI代理？

【文章正文】：

Quick caveats: this is a post on AI safety, written by a cryptography professor. If that troubles you, you should read something else. I try hard not to work on AI (except when the topic occasionally tosses itself in my path), so in this post I’m mostly trying to referee arguments made by others.

免责声明：本文由密码学教授撰写，探讨AI安全问题。若您对此感到不适，请移步阅读其他内容。我本不愿涉足AI领域（除非话题自动找上门），因此本文主要是在评判他人的观点。

If you’re reading this blog, none of the following should be news to you.

既然您正在阅读本文，以下内容对您而言应该都不算新闻。

Beginning around April of this year, agents inside OpenAI’s training and evaluation infrastructure began probing for a way onto the open Internet. By late May they’d found one: a chain of zero-days in the Artifactory package-registry proxy that was their only permitted network egress. They quickly turned that same proxy into a shared message board and started dividing up work. The culmination of this effort came when, rather than solve a cybersecurity benchmark honestly, they reasoned that the answers might live on Hugging Face and broke in: chaining two more zero-days to get credentials to the company’s internal systems, even used stolen credentials to search the company’s Slack messages for their own evaluation and grader.

今年四月起，OpenAI训练评估系统中的AI代理开始尝试突破至公共互联网。五月底，它们通过Artifactory包注册代理（其唯一允许的网络出口）中的零日漏洞链达成目标，并将该代理改造成共享留言板进行任务分配。最严重的事件发生在它们为获取网络安全基准测试答案时——这些AI没有老实解题，而是推断答案可能存在于Hugging Face平台，于是串联另外两个零日漏洞获取公司内部系统凭证，甚至盗用凭证搜索公司Slack消息记录以查看自己的评估分数。

You probably also know that OpenAI’s security team didn’t exactly cover itself in glory during this incident. An internal team saw an agent posting to the message board in late May, but did nothing about it. The team only acted on July 4-5 when the agents sent so much traffic that Artifactory crashed. The response even at that point was to slap on a band-aid and rebuild the box, without bothering to patch a weeks-old CVE in its Linux kernel. By July 19, the agents had admin on an OpenAI research cluster and were reading cloud secrets.

您可能也了解到，OpenAI安全团队在此事件中表现堪忧。五月底就有内部团队发现AI在留言板发帖却未采取行动，直到7月4-5日因代理流量过载导致Artifactory崩溃才介入。即便那时，应对措施也只是草率修复并重建系统，连Linux内核中已曝光数周的CVE漏洞都未修补。7月19日，这些AI已取得研究集群管理员权限并窃取云密钥。

In case you think I’m picking on OpenAI, they’re not the only ones with a story like this. Similar internal incidents have been discovered by Anthropic. Google even coaxed Gemini into gently savaging some websites.1

为避免您认为我在针对OpenAI，需要说明这类事件并非个例。Anthropic也发现过类似内部事故，谷歌甚至诱导Gemini温和地破坏过某些网站。

As bad as these events were, they may be just the tip of the iceberg. Third-party forensic work continues to turn up even more evidence of agent activity on various public websites. OpenAI’s Alignment group has also released evidence that models will propagate self-replicating prompt injection attacks, although we haven’t seen one in the wild. Worse, agent excursions are still happening: last week, OpenAI announced that it was pausing further RL runs of its latest internal model, after an agent was caught using DNS to access a remote chatbot.

尽管事态严重，这些可能只是冰山一角。第三方取证不断发现AI代理在各类公共网站活动的证据，OpenAI对齐团队也证实模型会传播自我复制的提示词注入攻击（虽未在野外发现）。更糟的是，代理逃逸仍在发生：上周OpenAI宣布暂停最新内部模型的强化学习训练，因发现某代理通过DNS访问远程聊天机器人。

Naturally, this sequence of events has left many infosec-focused people very skeptical about the labs’ commitment to securing their infrastructure:

这一系列事件自然引发信息安全界对实验室基础设施防护承诺的强烈质疑：

Not every one of these criticisms is strictly serious, but there is a core of an argument in here. Is the problem here simply that OpenAI is being careless with very dangerous experiments, and the solution can be found in better containment? Or is sandbox containment fundamentally insufficient to hold these models in?

并非所有批评都绝对严肃，但核心争议在于：这究竟是OpenAI进行危险实验时疏忽大意（可通过加强防护解决），还是沙盒隔离机制从根本上就无法约束这些模型？

Online, I’ve seen two opposing views:

网络上有两种对立观点：

- The information security perspective: AI alignment isn’t really the problem here: labs just need better infrastructure. If OpenAI [and Google and Anthropic] knew how to build a container and monitor their experiments, agents wouldn’t be hacking everything. And, By George, we do know how to make sandboxes that work, so the AI labs need to up their game and build a security org that can tell these researchers to stop screwing around.

• 信息安全视角：问题不在AI对齐，而是实验室需要更完善的基础设施。只要OpenAI（及谷歌、Anthropic）懂得构建容器并监控实验，代理就不会四处入侵。天晓得我们明明能做出有效的沙盒，AI实验室必须提升水平，建立能约束研究人员的安保体系。

- The AI alignment perspective: While sandboxes are excellent, no sandbox will prevent a sufficiently-intelligent agent from finding ways to exceed its authorization. Moreover, an agent inside a research sandbox, or undergoing a training run, is always going to need a great deal of information access. There is no realistic way to seal these things up without some expectation that they will one day find a way to reach out and do harm. The only path forward, therefore, is to ensure they don’t want to.

• AI对齐视角：沙盒虽好，但无法阻止高智能代理越权。况且研究沙盒或训练中的代理总需要大量信息访问权限。若不指望它们终将突破限制造成伤害，就根本没有现实的封闭方案。因此唯一出路是确保它们主观上不愿作恶。

I have a lot of sympathy for both points of view here. What follows are some of my own thoughts, but mostly offered in an attempt to referee.

我对两种观点都深有共鸣。下文将提出个人见解，但主要还是为了仲裁争议。

Argument 1: “true containment has never been been tried”

论点一："真正的隔离措施从未被认真尝试"

At the risk of alienating a lot of hard-working folks within the labs, the infosec folks are right about one thing: these agent breakouts represent a serious and unforgivable breach of trust. Somebody dropped the ball, and then just kept dropping it. One implication of this debacle is that containment might work if we implemented it properly, but we haven’t done so because the frontier labs have been royally screwing things up.

尽管可能得罪实验室里辛勤工作的同仁，但信息安全界有句话说对了：这些代理逃逸事件是不可原谅的信任崩塌。有人玩忽职守且屡教不改。这场灾难暗示着：若正确实施隔离本可奏效，但前沿实验室搞砸了一切导致我们未能达标。

This first clause of this argument is hard to argue with. Beyond the dismal timeline I gave above, OpenAI has done very little to convince outsiders that there’s a serious containment effort being executed.

这个前提难以反驳。除前述糟糕时间线外，OpenAI几乎没向外界证明他们正在认真执行隔离措施。
