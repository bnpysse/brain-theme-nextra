It's clear that Claire and others like her find their path through embracing emergence in their careers, rather than defining what they believe is their "life's work." They focus instead on aligning their decisions with two core principles—learning and making a significant impact. Here are the key takeaways from this concept:

### Embracing Emergence
1. **Principles-Based Decisions**: Rather than asking traditional questions like “Where do I want to go?” or “What is my life’s work?” individuals focus on identifying their guiding principles. Claire Hughes Johnson, for example, uses "learning" and "impact." This approach emphasizes the need to be self-aware and make decisions that align with one's values.
   
2. **Local Rules**: Develop personal guidelines based on what energizes you and what your core values are. These rules help in making daily decisions, such as whether to invest time or energy into a project or opportunity.

### Local Rules Framework
3. **Identify Energy Sources**: Focus on the activities that energize you—things that make you feel excited and engaged.
   
   - Use exercises like scoring your day with energy levels (1-10) and identifying peak moments where you felt most productive or fulfilled.
   - Engage a coach or friend to help identify these patterns and themes.

4. **Universal Principles**: These are the broader, more general principles that increase the likelihood of success in any career:

   1. **Be the Person Everyone Wants to Work With Again**:
      - **Build Trust and Credibility**: Ensure your work is of high quality, reliable, and consistent.
      - **Engage with Diverse People**: Surround yourself with a variety of people who challenge you and enrich your perspective.
      - **Create Positive Impact**: Aim for work that makes a difference in the world. It doesn't need to be about grand missions—it can start small but have significant impact.

### Practical Steps
1. **Reflect on Your Current Environment**:
   - Evaluate how you spend your days and nights—what energizes you, what frustrates you.
   - Think critically about why certain decisions felt right or wrong for you.

2. **Identify Your Strengths and Values**: 
   - What do you thrive in? What are the things that consistently bring out the best in you?
   - Write down these values to help guide your career choices going forward.

3. **Crafting Your Local Rules**:
   - Create a list of 10-20 guidelines that reflect your core principles (e.g., "I prioritize learning and impact," or "I seek diverse, respectful interactions").
   - Review and refine these rules regularly as you experience new situations and challenges in your career.

4. **Universal Principles Practice**:
   - Cultivate relationships: Make it a priority to spend time with and learn from people who challenge your perspectives.
   - Continuously engage in learning opportunities (e.g., courses, books, webinars) even if they are outside of your immediate professional sphere.
   - Strive for work that aligns with your values and contributes positively to the world.

### Conclusion
By defining your local rules based on self-awareness and energy patterns, and by embracing universal principles like seeking diverse interactions and making meaningful contributions, you can navigate a career path more aligned with your values and potential. This approach fosters adaptability and growth, leading to a fulfilling professional journey.

Implementing these strategies requires consistent reflection, practice, and adjustment as you encounter new experiences in both personal and professional spheres.
