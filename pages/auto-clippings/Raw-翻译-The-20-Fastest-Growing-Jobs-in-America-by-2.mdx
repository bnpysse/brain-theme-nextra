The 20 Fastest-Growing Jobs in America by 2035

Key Takeaways

Nurse practitioners are projected to be America’s fastest-growing occupation between 2025 and 2035, with employment rising 41%.

Healthcare accounts for 14 of the 20 fastest-growing occupations.

Renewable energy and technology also rank highly, with solar installers, wind turbine technicians, and data scientists all among the top four.

From healthcare and data science to solar and wind power, some of America’s fastest-growing careers span very different parts of the economy.

This visualization ranks the 20 U.S. occupations with the highest projected employment growth rates from 2025 to 2035. The data for this graphic comes from the

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

.

Importantly, these figures measure percentage growth rather than the absolute number of jobs created.

Healthcare Dominates America’s Fastest-Growing Jobs

The vast majority (70%) of occupations on the list are in healthcare, spanning roles from highly paid nurse practitioners and physician assistants to home health and personal care aides.

An aging U.S. population is one major factor supporting demand for healthcare and assistance services over the coming decade.

In fact, Americans age 65 and older are projected to

outnumber children

under 18 for the first time in 2029.

Clean Energy Jobs Are Growing Rapidly

Solar photovoltaic installers and wind turbine technicians rank second and fourth, respectively, with projected growth rates of 37% and 30%.

Both occupations are expanding as solar and wind capacity continues to grow across the country.

Because solar installers and wind turbine technicians start from smaller employment bases than many major occupations, rapid percentage growth does not necessarily translate into the largest number of new jobs.

Still, their positions near the top of the ranking highlight how the

changing energy mix

is creating new demand for skilled technical workers.

Wind, solar, and biofuels have expanded by more than 1,700% since 2000 and now account for 63% of U.S. renewable energy production.

Data and Cybersecurity Skills Remain in Demand

Technology also has a strong presence, with data scientists projected to grow by 35% through 2035.

Computer and information research scientists and information security analysts are expected to grow by 22% and 21%, respectively.

Technology also combines strong projected growth with some of the

highest salaries

on the list. Computer research scientists have the ranking’s highest median salary at $140,300, followed by information security analysts at $129,180 and data scientists at $120,230.

Rising demand for data analysis, advanced computing, and cybersecurity is helping sustain strong growth across these specialized occupations.
