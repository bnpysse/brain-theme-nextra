STS-51-F (also known as Spacelab 2) was the 19th flight of NASA's Space Shuttle program and the eighth flight of Space Shuttle Challenger. It launched from Kennedy Space Center, Florida, on July 29, 1985, and landed eight days later on August 6, 1985.

STS-51-F（又称空间实验室2号任务）是美国宇航局航天飞机计划的第19次飞行，也是挑战者号航天飞机的第8次飞行。该任务于1985年7月29日从佛罗里达州肯尼迪航天中心发射升空，八天后于1985年8月6日着陆。

While STS-51-F's primary payload was the Spacelab 2 laboratory module, the payload that received the most publicity was the Carbonated Beverage Dispenser Evaluation, which was an experiment in which both Coca-Cola and Pepsi tried to make their carbonated drinks available to astronauts.[1] A helium-cooled infrared telescope (IRT) was also flown on this mission, and while it did have some problems, it observed 60% of the galactic plane in infrared light.[2][3]

虽然STS-51-F的主要有效载荷是空间实验室2号实验舱，但最受公众关注的是碳酸饮料分配器评估实验——该实验中可口可乐和百事可乐都尝试让宇航员能在太空中饮用他们的碳酸饮料[1]。本次任务还搭载了氦制冷红外望远镜（IRT），尽管存在一些技术问题，该望远镜仍成功观测了60%银河系平面的红外图像[2][3]。

During launch, Challenger experienced multiple sensor failures in its Engine 1 Center SSME, which led to it shutting down. As a result, the shuttle had to perform an "Abort to Orbit" (ATO) emergency procedure. It is the only Shuttle mission to have carried out an abort after launching. As a result of the ATO, the mission was carried out at a slightly lower orbital altitude.

发射过程中，挑战者号1号主发动机（SSME）的多个传感器发生故障导致发动机关闭。航天飞机因此执行了"中止入轨"（ATO）应急程序，这是航天飞机计划中唯一一次在发射后执行中止程序。由于ATO操作，任务最终在略低于计划的轨道高度上实施。

Launch

[edit]

发射过程

[编辑]

STS-51-F's first launch attempt on July 12, 1985, was halted with the countdown at T−3 seconds after main engine ignition, when a malfunction of the number two RS-25 coolant valve caused an automatic launch abort. Challenger launched successfully on its second attempt on July 29, 1985, at 17:00 p.m. EDT, after a delay of 1 hour 37 minutes due to a problem with the table maintenance block update uplink.

STS-51-F在1985年7月12日的首次发射尝试中，主发动机点火后倒计时至T-3秒时，因二号RS-25发动机冷却阀故障触发自动发射中止。挑战者号在第二次尝试中于1985年7月29日美国东部时间17:00成功发射，由于表格维护块更新上行链路问题延迟了1小时37分钟。

At 3 minutes 31 seconds into the ascent, one of the center engine's two high-pressure fuel turbopump turbine discharge temperature sensors failed. Two minutes and twelve seconds later, the second sensor failed, causing the shutdown of the center engine. This was the only in-flight RS-25 failure of the Space Shuttle program. Approximately 8 minutes into the flight, one of the same temperature sensors in the right engine failed, and the remaining right-engine temperature sensor displayed readings near the redline for engine shutdown.
