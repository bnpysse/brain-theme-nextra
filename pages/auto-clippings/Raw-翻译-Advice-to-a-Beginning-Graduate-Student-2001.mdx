【文章正文】：
|  | Advice to a Beginning Graduate Student or What is Research? or The 4 R's of Graduate School: Reading, Rithmetic, Research, and Writing Outline of the talk: READING, STUDYING, THINKING,          STARTING OFF on the PhD, DEEP in the MIDDLE of the PhD, WRITING it all up. YOU  
《给研究生新生的建议》或《何为研究？》或《研究生院的4R法则：阅读、算术、研究与写作》演讲提纲：阅读、研习、思考、开启博士生涯、博士中期攻坚、论文撰写。你

Books are not scrolls. Scrolls must be read like the Torah from one end to the other. Books are random access -- a great innovation over scrolls. Make use of this innovation! Do NOT feel obliged to read a book from beginning          to end. Permit yourself to open a book and start reading from anywhere. 
书籍不是卷轴。卷轴必须像《托拉》般从头读到尾。书籍支持随机访问——这是超越卷轴的伟大创新。善用这个创新！不必强迫自己从头到尾阅读。允许自己打开书从任意处开始阅读。

In the case of mathematics or physics or anything especially hard, try          to find something anything that you can understand. Read what you can. Write in the margins. (You know how useful that can be.) Next time you come back to that book, you'll be able to read more. You can gradually learn extraordinarily hard things this way. 
对于数学、物理或其他特别艰深的内容，试着找到任何你能理解的部分。读你能读懂的内容。在页边写下批注。（你知道这有多有用。）下次重读时，你就能理解更多内容。通过这种方式，你可以逐步掌握异常困难的知识。

Consider writing what you read as you read it. This is especially true if you're intent on reading something hard. I remember a professor of Mathematics at MIT, name of BERTRAM KOSTANT, who would keep his door open whenever he was in his office, and he would          always be at his desk writing. Writing. Always writing. Was he writing up his research? Maybe. Writing up his ideas? Maybe. I personally think he was reading, and writing what he was reading. 
建议边读边写笔记。这在阅读艰深内容时尤为重要。我记得MIT有位数学教授BERTRAM KOSTANT，只要在办公室就开着门，永远伏案写作。写啊写。他是在撰写研究吗？或许。记录灵感吗？或许。我个人认为他是在阅读，并记录所读内容。

At least for me, writing what I read is one of the most enjoyable and          profitable ways to learn hard material.  
至少对我而言，边读边记是学习艰深材料最愉悦高效的方式。

You are all computer scientists. You know what FINITE AUTOMATA can do. You know what TURING MACHINES can do. For example, Finite Automata can add but not multiply. Turing Machines can compute any computable function. Turing machines are incredibly more powerful than Finite Automata. Yet the only difference between a FA and a TM is that the TM, unlike the FA, has paper and pencil. Think about it. It tells you something about the power of writing. 
你们都是计算机科学家。知道有限自动机（FA）能做什么。知道图灵机（TM）能做什么。例如FA能做加法不能做乘法，TM能计算任何可计算函数。图灵机比有限自动机强大得多。但两者唯一区别在于：TM有纸笔而FA没有。想想看。这揭示了写作的力量。

Without writing, you are reduced to a finite automaton. With writing you have the extraordinary power of a Turing machine. 
没有写作，你就退化为有限自动机。有了写作，你就拥有图灵机的超凡能力。

CLAUDE SHANNON once told me that as a kid, he remembered being stuck on          a jigsaw puzzle. His brother, who was passing by, said to him: "You know: I could tell you something." That's all his brother said. Yet that was enough hint to help Claude solve the puzzle. The great thing about this hint... is that you can always give it to yourself          !!! 
克劳德·香农曾告诉我，他小时候玩拼图卡住时，路过的哥哥对他说："知道吗，我可以提示你。"仅此一句。但这提示足以帮他解开谜题。这个提示的伟大之处在于...你永远可以给自己这样的提示！！！

I advise you, when you're stuck on a hard problem, to imagine a little birdie or an older version of yourself whispering "... I could tell you something..." 
我建议当你被难题困住时，想象有只小鸟或未来的自己在你耳边低语"...我可以提示你..."

I once asked UMESH VAZIRANI how he was able, as an undergraduate at MIT, to take 6 courses each and every semester. He said that he knew he didn't have the time to work out his answers the          hard way. He had to find a shortcut. You see, Umesh understood that problems often have short clever solutions. 
我曾问Umesh Vazirani如何在MIT本科期间每学期修六门课。他说知道自己没时间用笨办法解题，必须找捷径。你看，Umesh明白问题往往存在巧妙简解。

There will come a time when you work on a problem long and hard but UNsuccessfully          :( And then you learn that someone else found a solution. See this as the GREAT opportunity it is to learn something important. Don't let it pass you by. Ask yourself: "How SHOULD I have been thinking to solve that problem?" I have found that doing so is a powerful exercise. 
终有一天你会长期苦攻某个问题却失败:( 然后发现别人已解决。请将其视为学习重要知识的良机。别错过。自问："我本该用何种思路解决？"我发现这是极有效的训练。

Danny Sleator tells me that BOB FLOYD independently recommended exactly          this exercise to his students. He would lead them into asking themselves: "How COULD I have led myself to that answer?" Take the time to think it through. It's worth it. 
Danny Sleator告诉我，Bob Floyd也向学生推荐同样的训练。他会引导学生自问："我本该如何推导出这个答案？"花时间深入思考，这很值得。

There will come a time when you work on a problem long and hard and SUCCESSFULLY          :) And then you learn that someone else already published. :( Hard as that may be for you to take, you must view this too as a great          opportunity. Don't turn off. Read what got published. You will be surprised how often the published paper turns out to be different          in some significant way. 
终有一天你会长期苦攻某个问题并成功:) 然后发现别人已发表成果:( 虽然难以接受，但必须视之为良机。别气馁。研读已发表论文。你会惊讶地发现，很多情况下论文与你的成果存在重大差异。

Roughly 50% of the time, it is NOT at all the same as what you did. 25% of the time, it is the same but not as good. 25% of the time it is better. This means that 50% of the time or more, you can still publish. 
约50%的情况完全不同，25%雷同但不如你的成果，25%更优。这意味着至少50%的情况下你仍可发表。

And what about the 25% time that what got published is better than your          own? In that case, you have a great opportunity to learn. Ask yourself: "How SHOULD I have been thinking to solve the problem          in this fine way?" This is how I discovered, as a young engineer, that I should learn something          enormously powerful called "Modern Algebra." It's one reason I switched from Electrical Engineering as an undergraduate          major to Mathematics as a Graduate major. Of course, this was before there existed anything called Computer Science. 
那25%别人成果更优的情况呢？这正是绝佳的学习机会。自问："我本该如何想到这种精妙解法？"正是这样，年轻的我意识到该学习强大的"近世代数"，这也是我从本科电气工程转向研究生数学的原因之一——当然，那时还没有"计算机科学"这个学科。

继续谈思考...悖论与矛盾的重要性。当你能证明命题S为真，同时又能证明S为假时，你就知道触及了关键：某些根本性错误正潜藏其中...
