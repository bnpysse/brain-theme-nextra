Now That Information Is Free, What’s a College Campus For?

如今信息免费，大学校园还有什么用？

There is a strange contradiction unfolding in global education. As technology makes learning cheaper and increasingly detached from place, conventional wisdom says the physical university campus is becoming redundant. Knowledge is a few clicks away, and students can absorb a lecture, a course, or an entire degree’s worth of information from the comfort of their homes.

全球教育领域正呈现出一种奇怪的矛盾。随着技术让学习变得更廉价、越来越不受地域限制，传统观念认为实体大学校园正变得多余。知识只需点击几下即可获得，学生足不出户就能吸收一堂讲座、一门课程，甚至整个学位所需的信息。

India is doing almost the opposite. It is building more universities, expanding campuses, and increasingly attracting some of the world’s leading institutions to establish a physical presence within its borders. The Universities of Southampton, Liverpool, Bristol, and York, in the U.K., alongside the University of New South Wales, in Australia, are among those setting up campuses in India, a market that already serves more than 43 million higher education students, with enrollment growing significantly over the past decade.

印度却几乎反其道而行之。它正在建设更多大学、扩建校园，并日益吸引一些世界顶尖机构在其境内设立实体校区。英国的南安普顿大学、利物浦大学、布里斯托大学和约克大学，以及澳大利亚的新南威尔士大学，都在印度设立校区。印度高等教育市场已服务超过4300万学生，过去十年入学人数显著增长。

This is not simply a response to population scale, though scale is part of the story. India is projected to add hundreds of millions of urban residents by 2050, and its young population will continue to drive demand for education and opportunity. The more interesting story is not how many campuses India is building, but what those campuses are being built for. As the market evolves, there is a growing need for strategic business and health-centric training disciplines in the country, through high-performing universities and centers of excellence.

这不仅仅是对人口规模的回应，尽管规模是其中的一部分。预计到2050年，印度将新增数亿城市居民，其年轻人口将继续推动对教育和机会的需求。更有趣的故事不在于印度建了多少校园，而在于这些校园为何而建。随着市场的发展，该国通过高水平的大学和卓越中心，对战略性商业和以健康为核心的培训学科的需求日益增长。

For centuries, universities were organized around the delivery of knowledge. Classrooms, lecture halls, labs, and libraries existed because information was scarce and had to be physically delivered to those who wanted it. Today, the internet and AI have inverted that scarcity. Information is abundant, immediate, and increasingly personalized. A student no longer needs a room, a building, or even a campus to access what a professor knows.

几个世纪以来，大学围绕知识的传授而组织。教室、讲堂、实验室和图书馆之所以存在，是因为信息稀缺，必须通过实体方式传递给需要的人。如今，互联网和人工智能颠覆了这种稀缺性。信息丰富、即时，且日益个性化。学生不再需要一间房间、一栋建筑，甚至一个校园，就能获取教授所知道的知识。

But education has always been about more than information. Access to knowledge as a freedom of choice was never just about access to facts. It is about what happens between people once the facts are available: the unexpected conversation after a lecture, the argument in a corridor, the collaboration between students from entirely different disciplines who would never otherwise cross paths. Innovation tends to live in those intersections, not in the lecture itself.

但教育从来不仅仅是信息。作为选择自由的知识获取，从来不只是获取事实。它关乎事实可得之后人与人之间发生的事情：讲座后不期而遇的交谈、走廊里的争论、来自完全不同学科的学生之间的合作——这些学生若非如此永远不会相遇。创新往往存在于这些交汇点，而非讲座本身。

This reframes the architectural task. If a campus can no longer justify itself as the most efficient way to deliver information, it has to justify itself as something technology cannot replicate: a place built around belonging, mentorship, wellbeing, identity, and serendipity.

这重新定义了建筑设计的任务。如果校园不能再以传递信息的最有效方式自居，它就必须以技术无法复制的东西来证明自身价值：一个围绕归属感、导师指导、身心健康、身份认同和意外发现而构建的场所。

The pattern shows up in specific design decisions now underway across the country. On the new Institute of Integrated Learning in Management University (IILM) campus currently taking shape in Delhi, a new recreation building, designed by our firm, has been organized “inside-out,” with the library pushed to the building’s edge rather than buried at its center, and careful acoustic zoning so that energetic sport and quiet study can share the same structure without one drowning out the other. A short distance away, a new academic building (also by Gensler) designed to serve more than 4,000 students has been built with open, expandable floorplates, specifically so departments can reconfigure teaching space as disciplines evolve, rather than locking the university into a fixed layout for decades. None of this is decorative. It’s what happens when a campus is designed as infrastructure for belonging and adaptability rather than as a container for lectures.

这一模式体现在印度各地正在进行的具体设计决策中。在德里正在建设的新综合管理大学（IILM）校区，由我们公司设计的一座新娱乐建筑采用了“由内而外”的布局，将图书馆推到建筑边缘而非埋藏在中心，并精心规划声学分区，使充满活力的运动和安静的学习可以共享同一结构，而互不干扰。不远处，另一座由Gensler设计的学术建筑可容纳4000多名学生，采用开放、可扩展的楼面布局，专门让各院系随着学科发展重新配置教学空间，而不是让大学几十年锁定在固定布局中。这些都不是装饰性的。当校园被设计为归属感和适应性的基础设施，而非讲座的容器时，就会发生这样的变化。

Climate is also involved in this recalibration. A campus built for a 50-year lifespan in India must account for heat, monsoon intensity, and rising cooling demand in ways a climate-controlled, information-delivery building never had to. Passive design strategies, thicker envelopes, shaded circulation, and courtyards that pull air through a building rather than fight the climate with mechanical systems are becoming as central to campus design as any consideration of pedagogy. A building that cannot survive its own climate has little to offer the students it was built for, however well it is programmed. Increasingly, resilience and flexibility are treated as the same design problem: a campus has to be able to change with its students and endure its environment, and the buildings failing to do both are the ones already being reconsidered.

气候也参与这种重新校准。在印度，一座设计寿命为50年的校园必须考虑高温、季风强度和日益增长的制冷需求，而这些是气候受控、传递信息的建筑从未需要面对的。被动式设计策略、更厚的围护结构、遮阳的交通流线，以及能引导空气穿过建筑而非用机械系统对抗气候的庭院，正变得与任何教学法考量一样成为校园设计的核心。一座无法适应自身气候的建筑，无论其功能规划多么完善，对它所服务的学生来说都价值有限。越来越多地，韧性和灵活性被视为同一个设计问题：校园必须能够随学生而变化，并承受其环境，而那些无法同时做到这两点的建筑，正是已经被重新审视的对象。
