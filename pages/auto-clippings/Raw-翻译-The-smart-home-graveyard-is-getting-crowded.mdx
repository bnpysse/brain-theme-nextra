The smart home graveyard is getting crowded  
智能家居坟场正变得拥挤不堪  

The shutdown of June Oven is just another reminder that the cloud was never ours to keep.  
June智能烤箱的停运再次提醒我们：云端服务从来就不属于用户。  

How it started  
起源  
A decade ago, a group of former Apple engineers built a beautiful smart oven. With a built-in scale, restaurant-grade heating elements, a camera, and the intelligence to recognize a chicken breast and cook it perfectly, the June Oven set the standard for smart cooking appliances. At $1,495, it was absurdly expensive, but it quickly grew a small, devoted following that expanded when the company introduced a more modestly priced model.  
十年前，一群前苹果工程师打造了一款精美的智能烤箱。June烤箱凭借内置秤重、商用级加热元件、摄像头和智能识别鸡胸肉并精准烹饪的功能，树立了智能烹饪设备的标准。1,495美元的售价高得离谱，但它迅速积累了一批忠实拥趸，当公司推出价格更亲民的型号后，用户群体进一步扩大。  

This past Tuesday, June went dark. Weber, which bought the company in January 2021 and stopped making the ovens in 2022, shut off the app and cloud services, leaving its users with a sleek-looking toaster oven. There will be no app control, no software updates, and no new AI food recognition to crisp that strudel perfectly. You can still use it as an oven, but there will be no support or parts, and if you have to factory reset it for some reason, that could be the nail in the coffin. Weber told me it considered open-sourcing the technology for someone else to keep it alive, but decided against it, citing the fact June’s IP is part of its Weber Connect platform.  
上周二，June彻底熄火。2021年收购该公司的Weber（已于2022年停产烤箱）关闭了应用和云服务，用户只剩下一台外观时尚的烤面包机。再无应用控制、软件更新，也没有新AI食物识别功能来完美烤制酥皮卷。你仍可将其当作普通烤箱使用，但不会有任何技术支持或零部件供应——若因故需恢复出厂设置，可能就是压垮它的最后一根稻草。Weber向我表示曾考虑开源技术让他人维持服务，但最终因June的知识产权已融入Weber Connect平台而作罢。  

June is just the latest in a depressingly long list of connected products that have arrived in the smart home graveyard in the last two years alone. The Nest Secure, early Nest Thermostats, Belkin’s WeMo line, Neato’s robot vacuums, the Brava smart oven, Logitech’s Pop buttons, Bose’s SoundTouch speakers, and Sengled’s smart bulbs all lost their lifelines. While some were saved by local APIs and protocols — such as Zigbee, Thread, and Apple’s HomeKit, or a Home Assistant integration — most are like the June Oven, destined to live out their days in a zombie state.  
June只是智能家居坟场最新入驻者，过去两年间已有大量联网产品在此安息，名单长得令人沮丧：Nest安防系统、早期Nest恒温器、贝尔金WeMo系列、Neato扫地机器人、Brava智能烤箱、罗技Pop按钮、Bose SoundTouch音箱、生迪智能灯泡全都断了生命线。虽然部分产品因Zigbee/Thread协议、苹果HomeKit或Home Assistant集成等本地API得以续命，但多数都像June烤箱般沦为行尸走肉。  

How it’s going  
现状  
The silver lining, if you want to see it, is that most of these gadgets degrade rather than die outright. June and Brava will still work as ovens. Nest Thermostats can still control your heating and cooling, just like the $30 plastic wall wart they replaced, and a Neato will still vacuum your floors. But the remote access, voice control, updates, and other smart features you bought the devices for are gone. Some — like Nest Secure — are now pure e-waste.  
若非要寻找积极面，那就是多数设备只是功能降级而非完全报废。June和Brava仍能当普通烤箱使用，Nest恒温器依旧可以调控温度（就像它们取代的30美元塑料温控器），Neato还能打扫地板。但你购买这些设备时看重的远程控制、语音指令、系统更新等智能功能均已消失。有些产品（如Nest安防系统）则彻底沦为电子垃圾。  

There’s always the possibility of a rescue, but it’s rare. Some enterprising user may figure out a way to connect a headless corpse to Home Assistant, the open-source platform that has become the default refuge for the smart home undead. When smart lighting platform Insteon shut down in 2022, its users bought the company and resurrected it. Bose reversed course and open-sourced its SoundTouch API, and Pebble’s founder brought the iconic smartwatch back to life after the Rebble alliance kept it on life support. June’s co-founder Matt Van Horn said on X that he tried to keep the servers online but to no avail.  
复活并非全无可能，只是概率渺茫。某些极客用户或许能找到方法将"无头尸体"接入Home Assistant——这个开源平台已成为智能家居僵尸的默认收容所。2022年智能照明平台Insteon倒闭时，其用户集体收购公司使其重生；Bose转变策略开源SoundTouch API；Pebble创始人在Rebble联盟维持系统运转后，让这款标志性智能手表重获新生。June联合创始人Matt Van Horn在X上表示曾试图维持服务器运行，但最终徒劳无功。  

The smart home has reached a tipping point. Many of its early pioneers are aging out — a decade plus is a long time for a startup. Most have been bought by bigger companies, likely attracted by the shiny thing but then quickly dismayed by the bottom line. Back then, a cloud server was cheaper to build than a local hub, but servers continue to cost money, and once a company decides to shut down a product, reasons to keep paying fade rapidly. This appears to be what’s happening to Level Lock, another former-Apple-engineer startup that produced a beautiful replacement for a common household item. Assa Abloy bought the smart lock company and abruptly fired most of the team. It’s still alive, but how long it will continue is a big question.  
智能家居已到临界点。许多早期开拓者正退出舞台——对初创企业而言，十余年已是漫长周期。多数公司被大企业收购，后者或许曾被光鲜概念吸引，但很快被盈利现实击退。当年搭建云服务器比本地中枢更便宜，但服务器持续消耗成本，一旦公司决定停运产品，维持支出的理由便迅速消失。Level Lock似乎正经历同样命运——这个由前苹果工程师创立、生产精美家用锁具替代品的公司被Assa Abloy收购后，团队遭大规模裁员。虽然产品尚在运营，但能维持多久仍是未知数。  

What happens next  
未来出路  
Three things need to happen to prevent the smart home from burning more users: we need consumer protections; manufacturers need to step up and build in safeguards; we need to know how to protect ourselves.  
要避免智能家居继续伤害用户，需实现三件事：建立消费者保护机制、厂商主动构建保障措施、用户学会自我保护。  

Consumer Reports is pushing for legislation around connected IoT devices, including making manufacturers push a local API to a device before killing its cloud. This would allow it to keep talking to a platform like Home Assistant instead of becoming a paperweight. This is essentially what Bose did with SoundTouch, and other companies should follow that lead (I’m not holding my breath on legislation).  
《消费者报告》正推动物联网设备立法，要求厂商在关闭云服务前向设备推送本地API，使其能继续与Home Assistant等平台交互而非变成镇纸。Bose对SoundTouch的做法正是如此，其他公司应效仿（不过我对立法进程不抱期待）。
