【文章正文】：  
How often do you think about the 1893 World's Fair?  
你有多常想起1893年的世界博览会？  

Of all the displeasures that the Trump Administration has brought upon the District of Columbia, the most peculiar was the dinky little fairgrounds thrown up on the National Mall this past July.  
在特朗普政府给哥伦比亚特区带来的种种不快中，最奇特的是去年七月在国家广场上搭建的那个寒酸小展区。  

Crafted from the finest styrofoam and vinyl that misdirected donations can buy, its design unmistakably evoked the otherworldly classical ensembles of the 1893 World's Fair and its imitators.  
这个由被挪用捐款所能买到的最优质泡沫塑料和乙烯基材料打造的展区，其设计无疑让人联想到1893年世博会及其仿效者那些超凡脱俗的古典建筑群。  

In some ways, the choice is intuitive. The World's Columbian Exposition was an exuberant expression of American wealth, power, and identity that cultural creators have returned to with fascination again and again.  
从某种角度看，这个选择很直观。哥伦布世界博览会是美国财富、力量和身份的蓬勃展现，文化创作者们一再着迷地回溯这段历史。"让美国再次伟大"，对吧？  

But on the other hand, how did a festival that lasted only six months lodge itself so deeply in the American psyche?  
但另一方面，为何这个仅持续六个月的盛会能如此深植于美国人的集体心理？  

Why did we rebuild it in miniature on the National Mall?  
为何我们要在国家广场上重建它的微缩版本？  

Why has it become a feature of more than one conspiracy theory about a suppressed glorious past?  
为何它成了多个关于被压制辉煌历史的阴谋论的标志？  

Why is its discernment invoked on every can of Pabst Blue Ribbon?  
为何每罐Pabst蓝带啤酒都要援引它的鉴赏标准？  

Have we always been thinking about this plaster pop-up advertisement for American majesty?  
我们是否一直在惦记着这个为美国威严打造的石膏临时广告？  

The answer to all of these questions is... stranger than I had thought.  
所有这些问题的答案...比我想象的还要离奇。  

Whether thinking back to 1893 or 1993, what draws us to an event, story, or image depends less on the context of the events than the context we inhabit.  
无论是回溯1893年还是1993年，吸引我们关注某个事件、故事或图像的，与其说是事件本身的背景，不如说是我们所处的语境。  

As a result, we interpret—or reimagine—the past differently from our predecessors.  
因此，我们对过去的解读——或重新想象——与前辈们截然不同。  

And I think the lingering memory of the World's Columbian Exposition shows how it's from these reimaginings, not neutral facts, that we reshape the world.  
而我认为，哥伦布世界博览会挥之不去的记忆正表明：我们重塑世界的材料正是这些重新想象，而非客观事实。  

A New and Improved Jerusalem  
焕然一新的耶路撒冷  

One aspect of the fair that no historian will dispute is that, like the dinosaurs of Jurassic Park (1993), it blew people's minds.  
这场博览会有个无可争议的特点：就像《侏罗纪公园》(1993)里的恐龙一样，它震撼了所有人的心灵。  

Raves about the Exposition had begun even before it opened in May 1893 and continued well past its closure in October of that same year.  
早在1893年5月开幕前，对博览会的盛赞就已开始，并持续到同年10月闭幕后很久。  

Articles, telegrams, and diaries all testified to its wonder.  
文章、电报和日记都见证了它的神奇。  

For years after, both licensed and bootleg merchandise sold well, cluttering parlors into the new century.  
此后多年，无论是官方授权还是山寨商品都销路旺盛，将各式纪念品塞满了二十世纪初的客厅。  

Writers of all stripes have catalogued the diversity of its appeal.  
各类作家都记录过它多元化的吸引力。  

For a population that had only just become majority urban, Chicago was an attraction in and of itself.  
对当时刚刚实现城镇人口过半的美国民众而言，芝加哥本身就是个诱惑。  

Down in Hyde Park, the glass-roofed exhibit halls offered unbeatable publicity to manufacturers in an advertising environment of catalogues and newsprint.  
在海德公园，玻璃顶的展厅为制造商们提供了在目录册和新闻纸广告环境中无可匹敌的宣传平台。  

Over at the fair's Midway, visitors delighted in its carnival attractions while its ethnographic displays shook assumptions about humanity.  
而在博览会的娱乐区，游客们沉醉于嘉年华景点，同时人种学展览颠覆了他们对人类的认知。  

More than any of that, however, what lingered longer in people's memory was the central complex of the fair.  
但比这些更持久萦绕在人们记忆中的，是博览会的核心建筑群。  

Known officially as the "Court of Honor," and colloquially as the "White City," it was an ensemble of monumental plaster facades that shone in the daytime and glowed after dark in a lavish display of Westinghouse electric light—still radiant with the shock of the new.  
这个官方称为"荣誉法庭"、俗称"白城"的建筑群，由宏伟的石膏立面组成，白天熠熠生辉，入夜后在西屋电气公司提供的奢华灯光下流光溢彩——至今仍闪耀着令人震撼的新奇感。  

Hundreds of organizations held their annual meetings behind its facades and nearly a third of all Americans visited.  
数百家机构在这些建筑里举行年会，近三分之一的美国人曾亲临参观。  

It is not possible to trace every path that crossed in the White City.  
我们已无法追溯所有在白城交汇的人生轨迹。  

Some people were wowed and went home with the merch. Others were transformed.  
有人惊叹不已，带着纪念品回家；有人则被彻底改变。  

One such pilgrim was a journalist named Charles Mulford Robinson.  
记者查尔斯·穆尔福德·罗宾逊就是这样一位朝圣者。  

In the 1897 official history the fair, he described the experience of entering the Court of Honor as something like an ego death:  
在1897年的博览会官方历史中，他将进入荣誉法庭的体验描述为一种自我消亡：  

The faculties were all alert; he forgot himself or he felt the limits of his own personality slipping away, extending widely, boundlessly, until the whole scene was in his own soul.  
"所有感官都高度警觉；他忘却自我，或者说感到自我界限正在消融，无限延展，直至整个景象都融入他的灵魂。"  

That was the first, unanalyzed impression of the Fair; not the impression merely of the artist, the architect, or the poet, but of the everyday person, sounding infinite depths whose existence he never had known before.  
"这是对博览会最初、未经分析的印象；不仅是艺术家、建筑师或诗人的感受，也是普通人的体验——探测到了他此前从未知晓的无限深度。"  

He was far from alone in seeing the otherworldly in the design.  
认为这种设计具有超凡特质的不止他一人。  

In 1895, Frances Hogson Burnett, better known as the author of The Secret Garden, published a children's parable, Two Little Pilgrims' Progress, A Story of the City Beautiful.  
1895年，《秘密花园》作者弗朗西丝·霍奇森·伯内特出版了儿童寓言《两个小朝圣者的进步：美丽城的故事》。  

The short book follows two orphan children as they visit the fair while reading the 17th century Christian allegory Pilgrim's Progress.  
这本小书讲述两个孤儿边参观博览会，边阅读17世纪基督教寓言《天路历程》的故事。  

In Burnett's retelling, the spiritual journey is a physical one.  
在伯内特的改写中，精神之旅变成了实体旅程。  

The Celestial City that serves as Pilgrim's aim in the fable, it turns out, was right there on the shores of Lake Michigan, reached for reasonable fare.  
寓言中朝圣者的目标"天国城"，原来就在密歇根湖畔，只需支付合理费用即可抵达。  

Contemporary reviews of the book were mixed. None that I have found, however, objected to the comparison she made between this for-profit festival and actual literal Heaven.  
当时对这本书的评价褒贬不一。但我发现，没有人反对她将这个营利性盛会与真实的天堂相提并论。  

On to the next one  
下一个问题  

Here's another question. How much do you think about the Barcelona Summer Olympics? Woodstock '94? Michael Jackson's halftime show at Super Bowl XXVII?  
再问个问题：你有多常想起巴塞罗那夏季奥运会？1994年伍德斯托克音乐节？迈克尔·杰克逊在第27届超级碗的中场表演？  

Each of these brief events from roughly thirty years ago was deeply influential in its own time and on the industries they were part of.  
这些大约三十年前的短暂事件，都在其所属时代和领域产生过深远影响。  

Yet I think it's safe to say that they don't hold our memory as well as media from 1993 like Whitney Hous...  
但可以说，它们在我们记忆中的留存度，远不及1993年的惠特尼·休斯...（原文截断）
