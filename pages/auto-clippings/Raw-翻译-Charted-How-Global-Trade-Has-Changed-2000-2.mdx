Charted: How Global Trade Has Changed (2000–2025)

Key Takeaways

Global merchandise imports and exports totaled a combined $51.4 trillion in 2025, roughly four times their value in 2000.

Electronics and electrical goods were the largest category, accounting for $12.1 trillion in combined trade flows.

Minerals, metals, and related products grew 5.6 times since 2000, the fastest increase among the major categories shown.

Over the past quarter-century, global goods trade has expanded sharply, while its category mix has also shifted.

The $51.4 trillion figure combines merchandise imports and exports, meaning it captures both sides of international trade. By comparison, the World Trade Organization (WTO) values world merchandise trade at $26.3 trillion in 2025 when measured by exports alone.

Electronics Have Become the Backbone of Global Trade

Combined imports and exports of electronics and electrical goods reached $12.1 trillion in 2025, up from $3.2 trillion in 2000. The category accounts for nearly one-quarter of the total shown.

At $12.1 trillion, electronics exceed second-place minerals and metals by about $4.8 trillion, roughly equal to the entire value of transport equipment shown.

Behind that scale is a vast network of semiconductors, smartphones, electrical components, and precision instruments used across everything from cars and factories to data centers.

In 2025, trade in AI-enabling goods such as semiconductors and processors reached nearly

$4.2 trillion

, driving 42% of global merchandise trade growth.

Minerals and Metals Have Grown Even Faster

Electronics dominate by value, but

minerals

and metals have grown the fastest.

Combined trade flows in the category increased by a 5.6 multiple between 2000 and 2025, rising from $1.3 trillion to $7.3 trillion. Agriculture and food followed at 5.2 times their 2000 level.

The gains extend well beyond high-tech products. Trade in metals, minerals, and

agricultural commodities

has been supported by rising industrial and consumer demand worldwide.
