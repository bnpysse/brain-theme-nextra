Why Another Think Tank?

Jordan Schneider:

Saif Khan, at long last, welcome to ChinaTalk. Why does the world need another think tank? What is the appetite right now for evidence-based policy?

Saif Khan:

There is, surprisingly, a gap in the AI policy think tank ecosystem that we’re trying to fill. Industry and foundations are gobbling up lots of talent, but there’s still a lot of space to do impactful research at think tanks in DC. I would encourage people not to give up on that.

We have our own special spin on what’s important. Back in my days at Georgetown CSET, before anybody really cared about AI policy or even semiconductor policy, we had all the time in the world to think about topics that would be really important in the coming years and to have policy ideas on the shelf for when the policy window opened. Today, every single day brings a new piece of news on AI, on policy, on chips so there’s less space these days for forward-looking research. We’re trying to fill a bit of a gap in the ecosystem by putting our heads down and coming up with new ideas that will matter on a one-to-three-year timeframe.

The other thing we’re trying to be a central home for: some of the most productive policy research of the last few years has come from understanding the AI supply chain — from chipmaking tools to tokens to robots. Understanding what is happening in the physical world grounds research in a way that makes it much more impactful. We talk about this in the

launch essay

on our website. Understanding physically what’s happening in the real world of AI helps you identify patterns that other people don’t see, and you can also predict how the world is going to unfold. I spent a lot of time doing semiconductor policy research at CSET, and there’s a predictability to understanding supply chains: if you move a policy lever, you can see how the world will unfold.

If you’re looking at something empirically grounded, there’s also almost a fractal-like element. You can dig into the details, and you’ll always learn more. It’s time that’s never wasted. You’re not researching angels dancing on the head of a pin — these are real-world things. We’re trying to be a hub of that kind of work, which is still relatively underserved by people with the technical expertise to do it. And regardless of how you feel about the policy window right now, there are going to be points in time when the forward-looking piece matters.

Jordan Schneider:

It’s interesting to think about the evolution of this field and what gaps remain. I remember conversations with you when you were at Commerce, when you’d ask, “Jordan, what’s up with this China AI stuff?” and I’d say, “I mean, there are three people staring at it.” That was 2023. That is no longer the case, which I feel almost relieved about. We’ve gone from four people whose full-time job it is to serve the English-speaking policy community looking at China and AI to, I don’t know, thirty or fifty. The government has also clued in, in a way that was not the case in the pre-DeepSeek days.

But I see your point that now there’s all this noise. The two investing styles of doing policy work, let’s call them, are having longer horizons and looking into physical stuff. The thing I’ve always been scared of is that the policy window closes before the research is done. But you’re right that there’s a need for both.

为什么还要再建一个智库？

Jordan Schneider:

Saif Khan，终于等到你了，欢迎来到ChinaTalk。为什么世界还需要另一个智库？目前对基于证据的政策的需求有多大？

Saif Khan:

令人惊讶的是，AI政策智库生态系统中确实存在一个我们试图填补的空白。产业界和基金会正在吸纳大量人才，但在华盛顿特区的智库中，仍有很大的空间去做有影响力的研究。我会鼓励人们不要放弃这一点。

对于什么是重要的，我们有自己独特的看法。回想我在乔治城大学CSET的日子，那时还没有人真正关心AI政策甚至半导体政策，我们有充足的时间去思考未来几年真正重要的议题，并储备政策想法，等待政策窗口打开。如今，每天都有关于AI、政策、芯片的新消息，所以留给前瞻性研究的空间更少了。我们试图通过埋头苦干，提出在一到三年时间框架内重要的新想法，来填补生态系统中的一些空白。

我们试图成为另一个核心家园：过去几年一些最有成效的政策研究来自于对AI供应链的理解——从芯片制造工具到代币再到机器人。理解物理世界中正在发生的事情，使研究有了根基，从而更具影响力。我们在网站上的

创刊文章

中谈到了这一点。理解AI现实世界中正在发生的物理情况，有助于你识别其他人看不到的模式，你也能预测世界将如何发展。我在CSET花了很多时间做半导体政策研究，理解供应链有一种可预测性：如果你移动一个政策杠杆，你就能看到世界将如何演变。

如果你关注的是有实证基础的东西，它几乎还有类似分形的元素。你可以深入细节，总会学到更多。时间永远不会浪费。你研究的不是针尖上跳舞的天使——这些都是现实世界的事物。我们试图成为这类工作的中心，而这类工作仍然相对缺乏具备技术专长的人来做。而且，无论你目前对政策窗口有何看法，总会有一些时刻，前瞻性的部分会变得重要。

Jordan Schneider:

思考这个领域的演变以及还存在哪些空白是很有趣的。我记得你在商务部时我们的一些对话，你会问：“Jordan，中国AI这事儿怎么样了？”我会说：“我的意思是，有三个人在盯着。”那是2023年。现在情况已不再如此，我几乎感到松了一口气。我们已经从四个全职为英语政策界服务、关注中国和AI的人，变成了，我不知道，三十或五十个。政府也已经意识到了这一点，这在DeepSeek出现之前是不存在的。

但我明白你的观点，现在有太多噪音。做政策工作的两种投资风格，我们姑且这么叫，一是拥有更长的视野，二是关注实体事物。我一直害怕的是，政策窗口在研究完成之前就关闭了。但你说得对，两者都需要。
