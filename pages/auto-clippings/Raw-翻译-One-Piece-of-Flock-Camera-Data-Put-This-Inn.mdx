One Piece of Flock Camera Data Put This Innocent Woman in Jail for 13 Days  
一条Flock摄像头数据让无辜女性蒙冤入狱13天  

Police didn't care that Lindsey Isaacs' car was the wrong color, and wasn't damaged. They still arrested her because of Flock data.  
警方无视林赛·艾萨克斯的车辆颜色不符且毫无损毁的事实，仅凭Flock数据就实施了逮捕。

In the months since Flock cameras have helped put an easily recognized and hated name to the broader threat of a deeply repressive technological surveillance state–Darth Vader activist performance art and all–I’ve been waiting for a story to come along that would truly crystalize the stakes involved here.  
数月来，Flock摄像头作为技术监控国家的压迫象征（堪比达斯·维达式的行为艺术），我一直期待一个能彻底揭露其危害性的案例。  

Flock cameras, and the tech they now culturally represent, automated license plate readers (ALPRs), are increasingly hated in bipartisan fashion, but it hasn’t felt like there’s been that one news story that could succinctly demonstrate for the average person exactly how dangerous the technology can be for personal liberty. Until now, that is.  
Flock摄像头及其代表的自动车牌识别技术（ALPRs）正遭到两党共同抵制，但始终缺乏一个让普通人直观理解技术如何威胁自由的典型案例——直到现在。  

Because what else would you call the account of a woman who was arrested by incompetent police based on nothing more than a single Flock camera piece of data, and held in jail for 13 days (partially in solitary confinement) until they realized it was definitely the wrong person?  
当一名女性仅因一条Flock数据就被无能警方逮捕，羁押13天（含单独监禁）后才发现抓错人——这难道不是最骇人听闻的例证？  

You couldn’t ask for a more egregious demonstration of how this technology is being abused by police who need no additional help in trampling over American civil liberties.  
无需更多佐证，这已完美展示技术如何被警方滥用，他们本就擅长践踏美国公民自由。  

The woman in question is named Lindsey Isaacs. She’s a 23-year-old resident of Palm Beach, Florida.  
当事人是23岁的佛罗里达州棕榈滩居民林赛·艾萨克斯。  

One morning in October of 2025, she woke up at 2 a.m. to find that state troopers were outside her apartment, and a tow truck was currently in the process of confiscating her car, a black Dodge Durango.  
2025年10月某日凌晨2点，她发现州警包围公寓，拖车正没收她的黑色道奇Durango。  

The police had reportedly used images and data captured by a Flock ALPR camera to connect Isaacs and her car to a deadly car accident that had happened one day earlier, which had resulted in the loss of three lives.  
警方据称利用Flock摄像头数据，将艾萨克斯与前日致三人死亡的肇事逃逸案关联。  

According to witnesses at the scene, the deadly collision had been perpetrated by a Dodge Durango. The Flock camera, meanwhile, had recorded Isaacs’ car and its license plate several miles away from the site of the accident, sometime around the time of the incident.  
目击者称肇事车为道奇Durango，而Flock摄像头记录到艾萨克斯的车辆在案发时段出现在数英里外。  

“They said, ‘We have your plate on a Flock camera, and your car has damage consistent with a collision,'” said Isaacs this week, now testifying before Congress. “And I said, ‘Where’s the damage? You’ve got the wrong person.'”  
"警方说'Flock拍到你的车牌，车辆有碰撞痕迹'，"本周在国会作证的艾萨克斯回忆，"我反问'痕迹在哪？你们抓错人了'。"

Isaacs was right. It should have taken only a cursory examination of her car to see that hey, this vehicle really didn’t look like one that had been part of an accident 24 hours earlier that killed three people!  
艾萨克斯是对的。但凡粗略检查就能发现：这辆车根本不像24小时前致三人死亡的肇事车！  

And oh, wait, do we think it’s relevant that the eyewitnesses were saying that it had been a maroon Durango that had been involved in the incident, and Isaacs’ car was instead black?  
等等！目击者指认的是栗色Durango，而艾萨克斯的车是黑色——这难道不重要？  

Nevertheless, her car was instead entered as evidence into a case and impounded, and Florida Highway Patrol issued a warrant for her arrest on April 17, 2026, some SEVEN MONTHS LATER.  
然而车辆仍被列为证据扣押。七个月后的2026年4月17日，佛罗里达公路巡警签发逮捕令。  

This, despite the fact that there appears to have been zero physical evidence actually linking Isaacs to the scene of the crime besides a Flock camera sighting indicating she had been nearby that day, and this despite the fact that her car was not actually damaged, something that police had seven months to investigate and ascertain.  
尽管除Flock记录外毫无物证，尽管警方有七个月时间核实车辆完好无损。  

She would spent the next two weeks in a maximum security jail housing, including 86 consecutive hours that she spent in solitary confinement.  
她在高度戒备监狱度过两周，其中86小时连续单独监禁。  

She told the U.S. Senate in her testimony, meanwhile, that correctional officers told her she was being put in solitary because of “the severity of the charges.” You know, to the crime she did not commit.  
她在参议院作证时透露，狱警称单独关押是因"指控严重性"——针对她根本没犯的罪。  

“I was terrified,” Isaacs understandably said to U.S. legislators this week. “I was facing the possibility of spending the rest of my life in prison for a crash that I knew I had not been involved in. I did not know if I would ever get out of jail. At my lowest point, I didn’t want to be alive.”  
"我吓坏了，"艾萨克斯对议员们说，"可能因莫须有罪名终身监禁。最绝望时，我甚至不想活下去。"  

Yeah! I can understand a person feeling completely hopeless and utterly abandoned by not only an uncaring but actively misanthropic justice system after being arrested and charged with eight felonies, including three counts of vehicular homicide, when you know that you had nothing to do with it!  
是啊！被控八项重罪（含三项车辆杀人罪）却清白无辜，面对冷漠甚至恶意的司法系统，谁能不绝望？  

I can understand the utter rage that Isaacs must have been feeling during each day she sat behind bars, wondering aloud why the Florida Highway Patrol wasn’t doing the BARE MINIMUM of investigation of its own into this case, such as noticing that the so-called murder weapon of her vehicle had not actually been involved in a crash.  
完全理解艾萨克斯在狱中的愤怒：为何佛州巡警连最基本的调查都不做？比如核实所谓"凶器"车辆根本无碰撞痕迹。  

What is an average citizen supposed to do when facing a criminal justice apparatus that simply doesn’t give a shit, and one that is so eager to rely on A...  
当刑事司法系统漠不关心且盲目依赖AI时，普通公民能怎么办？
