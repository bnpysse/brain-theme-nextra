From his voice it's clear that something is very wrong; he sounds nothing like his usual self. He's due the next day to meet us for an interview in Paducah, Kentucky, where he and his friend Chuck Deuschle worked at a uranium-enrichment plant that helped fuel America's nuclear arsenal. Years ago they blew the whistle on the government and its contractors for poisoning thousands of atomic workers. "Can't make it," Fowler chokes out. "Blood clots. Hospital. They may have to remove some toes—if those clots don't reach my heart first. If they do..." He trails off, the terrible ending hanging in the air.
他的声音明显不对劲，与平日判若两人。原本约定次日要在肯塔基州帕迪尤卡接受我们采访——他和老友查克·多伊彻曾在那座为美国核武库提供燃料的铀浓缩工厂工作。多年前他们揭发政府及承包商毒害数千名核工人的行径。"我来不了了，"福勒艰难地挤出话语，"血栓...住院...可能要截掉几根脚趾——如果血栓没先到心脏的话。要是到了..."话音戛然而止，可怕的结局悬在沉默中。

Fowler, soon to turn 77, is desperate to talk before it's too late—to pull up the memories one more time and commit them to the historical record. He and Deuschle had both moved to Kentucky to work at the nuclear plant, only to discover a grim secret: The facility was exposing people to dangerously high levels of radiation and toxic chemicals, and had concealed the truth about it from employees and the surrounding community. This went on for decades. Faced with a choice—either walk away or stay and sound the alarm—Fowler and Deuschle returned to work, day after day.
即将77岁的福勒迫切要在为时已晚前开口——最后一次打捞记忆，将其镌刻进历史。他和多伊彻迁居肯塔基州进入核工厂后，竟发现骇人秘密：该设施让员工和周边社区暴露在危险的高辐射与有毒化学物质中，并隐瞒真相数十年。面对"抽身离去"或"留下警示"的抉择，他们选择日复一日回到工作岗位。

More than thirty years later, lying in the hospital with cancer, Fowler is certain that the plant doomed him to a slow, painful death. At 83, Deuschle is also fighting a serious health issue: an unexplained autoimmune disease. They believe their fellow whistleblowers—Deuschle's wife, Kay, and their friend Garland "Bud" Jenkins—succumbed to illnesses caused by the plant. Still, they'd do it all again. Someone had to reveal the callous disregard the government and the plant's operators had shown their own people.
三十多年后的今天，罹患癌症住院的福勒确信工厂注定让他缓慢痛苦地死去。83岁的多伊彻也在对抗不明原因的自身免疫疾病。他们相信其他吹哨人——多伊彻的妻子凯伊和友人加兰·"巴德"·詹金斯——皆因工厂引发的疾病去世。但若重来，他们依然会这么做。必须有人揭露政府与工厂运营者对民众的冷酷漠视。

Fowler and Deuschle have never shared their story in full—the details of what it was like to live, breathe, and possibly die from their commitment to the truth. But they want people to know, particularly in light of recent news reports: In August 2025, more than a decade after the plant was shut down, a private firm announced that it planned to revive uranium-enrichment operations there. To Fowler and Deuschle, it seems wrong to begin a new chapter in Paducah's nuclear story. Many former employees are still seeking compensation for their exposure to radiation. Will future generations of workers be any safer?
福勒与多伊彻从未完整讲述过他们为真相呼吸、生存乃至可能死亡的细节。但鉴于2025年8月某私营企业宣布计划重启该铀浓缩工厂（已关停十余年），他们认为必须警示世人。对这两位老人而言，在帕迪尤卡核故事中开启新篇章实属荒谬。众多前员工仍在为辐射伤害寻求赔偿，未来工人的处境真能改善吗？

Fowler struggles for each breath.

"时间到了，"护士轻声说，伸手要拿回电话，"您需要休息。"

第一章
One morning in late 1991, Ron Fowler stood in front of the bathroom mirror, parting his hair to the side and noting the first strands of gray at his temples. His beard was neatly trimmed, his black slacks and white dress shirt crisply pressed. He was ready. Today would be his first day at the Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant, a uranium-enrichment facility about ten miles outside Paducah, Kentucky. This was more than just a job—it was the start of a new life.
1991年末某个清晨，罗恩·福勒站在浴室镜前将头发侧分，注意到鬓角初现的银丝。胡须修剪得体，黑色休闲裤与白衬衫熨烫笔挺。他已准备就绪——今天是他入职帕迪尤卡气体扩散厂（肯塔基州郊外十英里处的铀浓缩设施）的首日。这不仅是一份工作，更是新生活的开端。

Fowler was a health physicist, an expert in the science of radiation protection. For years he'd traveled the country to train workers at nuclear plants in safety protocols. The pay was great, but being on the road weeks at a time had worn him down. When he moved to Paducah in his early forties, Fowler had already lived in Illinois, Pennsylvania, Maryland, Virginia, and Arkansas. Paducah promised stability: the perfect place to settle through retirement. His wife's parents even moved from Illinois to be nearby.
作为辐射防护专家，福勒常年奔波于各地核工厂开展安全培训。高薪背后是长期出差的身心俱疲。四十出头迁居帕迪尤卡前，他已在伊利诺伊、宾夕法尼亚等五州居住。这座小城象征着安定——理想的退休终老之地。岳父母甚至特地从伊利诺伊州搬来毗邻而居。

After eating breakfast—a sausage patty and a scrambled egg—he kissed his wife, Sandi, goodbye and slipped out the door. He steered his green Oldsmobile Delta 88 through the country roads outside Paducah and let his mind drift back to his childhood in Florence, South Carolina. Life there had been simple. His father, who dropped out of school in the fourth grade, supported the family by working on the railroad. Florence was a place where men had jobs, women tended to the home and children, everyone went to church, and neighbors helped neighbors who fell on hard times. Paducah felt just as close-knit. Folks looked out for one another, and honest labor was rewarded.
吃完香肠煎蛋早餐，他吻别妻子桑迪出门。驾驶绿色奥兹莫比尔88型轿车穿行乡间公路时，思绪飘回南卡罗来纳州佛罗伦萨的童年。那时生活纯粹：四年级辍学的父亲靠铁路工作养家。在佛罗伦萨，男人工作、女人持家、周日礼拜、邻里互助。帕迪尤卡同样充满温情，人们相互照应，勤劳终得回报。
