【文章标题】：Future of Cancer Care Today (FCCT)

【文章正文】：

There is more science available to cancer patients than what one care team can keep up with. New diagnostics, treatments, trials, and suppliers keep emerging, and when standard of care runs out, patients are usually left to figure out what else might be possible on their own.

Future of Cancer Care Today (FCCT) helps cancer patients and their families connect the dots. Our PhD-led team surfaces diagnostic and treatment options relevant to a patient's case, including those beyond standard of care, and finds vetted suppliers and providers who work beyond standard protocols. FCCT does not replace doctors or make treatment decisions. We create playbooks and curate resources to help families ask better questions, so they can walk into the next conversation with a fuller picture.

Free for patients.

Important to know.

Future of Cancer Care Today is not a medical provider. We do not diagnose, prescribe, or direct treatment. We provide a non-clinical support layer focused on information, education, and decision support, intended to complement your relationship with your care team. Some materials may be generated with the support of artificial intelligence and should be reviewed with your medical providers before informing any care decisions. All decisions regarding your care belong to you and your doctor.

Patient-First Data and Tissue Release

One standing authorization US patients can use to request or direct the release of records, imaging, test data, and available tissue to recipients they choose.

We invite patients, and the organizations that generate or hold their data or tissue, to use the release and share feedback on how it works in practice. This will help us make the form clearer, easier to process, and more effective for everyone involved.

Why This Form Is Needed

Patients can usually obtain clinical notes and summary reports. Obtaining the underlying materials can be more difficult. These may include original imaging files, pathology materials, sequencing files, laboratory data, and available tissue. They may be needed for a second opinion, additional testing, clinical trial evaluation, or research.

What the Form Does

- Identifies who is authorizing the release and where the materials are held. The form includes the patient’s information, the authority of any personal representative, and multiple releasing institutions and departments.

- Defines the records and data covered. This includes medical and billing records; laboratory, pathology, and imaging reports; original imaging files; electronic health information exports; genomic and laboratory reports; raw data files; and related assay and analysis information. The authorization covers all dates.

- Includes available tissue and other specimens. Covered materials include pathology slides and tissue blocks, as well as blood, plasma, serum, bone marrow, and other biological materials.

- Addresses sensitive information separately. The patient must initial the categories they authorize for release, including genetic or genomic, HIV/AIDS-related, mental health, and reproductive or sexual health information. Psychotherapy notes are excluded. Substance use disorder records covered by 42 C.F.R. Part 2 require separate consent.

- Defines who may receive the materials. The authorization covers specified healthcare, laboratory, data exchange, and research recipients, as well as another person or organization identified by the patient. The recipient may tell the releasing institution where to send the materials.

- Creates a standing authorization. Once the form is on file, an authorized recipient may request covered materials without obtaining a new patient signature for each request. The authorization remains effective for up to 99 years unless the patient revokes it or applicable law requires an earlier expiration.

- Explains the limits of releasing physical materials. Tissue and other specimens can be released only when they remain available and release is permitted. Materials that were discarded or not preserved cannot be recovered. A release may also reduce what remains for future care or testing.

- Includes standard authorization terms. These address purpose, expiration, revocation, possible re-disclosure, voluntariness, permitted fees, the right to a copy, governing law, and signatures. The form is intended to satisfy HIPAA authorization requirements under 45 C.F.R. § 164.508.

Requests remain subject to applicable law, availability, and institutional policy. The form covers the release of the materials. Later use or sharing by the recipient may require a separate agreement.
