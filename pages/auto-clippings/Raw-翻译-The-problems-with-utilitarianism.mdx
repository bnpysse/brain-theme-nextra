【文章标题】：The problem(s) with utilitarianism  
功利主义的问题  

【文章正文】：  
Much to my amusement, the world seems to have discovered Effective Altruism. As the debate over AI safety and AI regulation has heated up, the national press is finally paying attention to the odd cultural bubble within the tech industry and tech-related circles of San Francisco Bay Area culture that produced much of the thinking on the topic up until now.  
令我颇感有趣的是，世界似乎突然发现了"有效利他主义"。随着关于AI安全和AI监管的辩论升温，主流媒体终于开始关注旧金山湾区科技产业及相关文化圈中那个催生了大量相关理论的怪异文化泡沫。  

For many years, Effective Altruism — or EA, as it’s known — was a cross between a Berkeley technohippie cult and a fun intellectual sideshow that offered nerds a pleasant distraction from whatever people in mainstream politics were yelling at each other about.  
多年来，有效利他主义（简称EA）就像是伯克利技术嬉皮士崇拜与趣味智力杂耍的混合体，为书呆子们提供了逃避主流政治争吵的愉快消遣。  

Part of that involved thinking a lot about the future of artificial intelligence, and a lot of the tech people who would end up working at AI companies — especially Anthropic — were very influenced by those discussions.  
其中部分讨论涉及对人工智能未来的大量思考，最终进入AI公司（尤其是Anthropic）的许多科技从业者都深受这些讨论影响。  

So now that AI is the most important thing in the world, people in the outside world are discovering this strange little corner of our culture for the first time, and it’s very amusing to watch.  
如今AI成为全球最重要议题时，外界才第一次发现我们这个文化中的奇怪角落，观察这个过程非常有趣。  

You have mainstream news outlets publishing things like "This well-meaning ideology fueling AI panic has a dark side", "How Effective Altruism Took Over the World", "The Dangerous Ideology Behind the AI Warnings", and so on.  
主流媒体开始刊登诸如《助长AI恐慌的善意意识形态暗藏阴暗面》《有效利他主义如何征服世界》《AI警告背后的危险意识形态》等文章。

因此，人们开始真正关注有效利他主义社区里某些更极端的观点，比如博客"边沁的斗牛犬"提出的：  

**Bentham's Newsletter**  
**边沁通讯**  

**Insects Matter More Than People**  
**昆虫比人类更重要**

The notion that insects, in the aggregate, matter more than human beings received a huge amount of scorn.  
"昆虫总体比人类更重要"的观点遭到了大量嘲讽。  

But in fact, thinking about insect welfare versus human welfare is an important mental exercise, because it demonstrates some of the fundamental flaws in utilitarianism.  
但事实上，将昆虫福利与人类福利进行对比是重要的思维训练，它揭示了功利主义的一些根本缺陷。  

Those flaws were minor side-notes up until now, but in the age of AI they’re going to become increasingly uncomfortable and glaring.  
这些缺陷过去只是次要问题，但在AI时代将变得越来越令人不安且显眼。  

As an economist, I was inculcated with utilitarianism from day 1. The notion that people ought to get what they want underlies essentially all of modern economic thinking.  
作为经济学家，我从入行第一天就被灌输功利主义思想。"人们应该得到想要的东西"这一观念构成了现代经济学的基石。  

GDP, at its most simple, is a measure of the degree to which people in our society get what they want.  
简而言之，GDP就是衡量社会成员获取所欲之物程度的指标。  

In some sense, economists adopted utilitarianism as a sort of defensive measure — instead of going out on a limb and endorsing some politically contentious concept of virtue or justice or national greatness or whatever, they could just say "We just want to give people what they want."  
某种程度上，经济学家采用功利主义是种防御策略——与其冒险支持某些具有政治争议的美德、正义或国家伟大等概念，不如直接说"我们只想满足人们的欲望"。  

But even though it felt like a bare-bones sort of moral vision, valuing humanity’s aggregate utility can have some pretty strong policy implications.  
尽管这像是最低限度的道德愿景，但重视人类总体效用会产生强烈的政策影响。  

Elon Musk wouldn’t even notice if his bank account went up or down by a billion dollars. But to plenty of regular people, a hundred thousand dollars would be life-changing.  
埃隆·马斯克根本不会注意到银行账户增减十亿美元，但对许多普通人来说，十万美元就能改变人生。  

So why not take $1 billion out of Elon’s bank account and parcel it out to 10,000 random working-class people? Elon wouldn’t even notice, and the 10,000 people would get a lot more of what they want in life — better health care, an education for their kids, nicer food, a bigger house, or whatever. Utilitarianism is the foundation of redistribution.  
那么何不从马斯克账户取出十亿美元分给一万名随机工薪阶层？马斯克不会察觉，而这一万人将获得更多人生所需——更好的医疗、子女教育、美味食物、大房子等。功利主义就是再分配的基础。  

How far can you push that principle, though? The above example — redistributing just 0.1% of the wealth of the world’s richest man — seems clear enough.  
但这个原则能推行到什么程度？上述例子——重新分配世界首富0.1%的财富——似乎很合理。  

But you can just keep designing more extreme thought experiments, until you end up asking whether bugs’ lives matter more than humans’ lives, and everyone just starts laughing at you.  
但你可以不断设计更极端的思想实验，直到开始质疑"昆虫生命是否比人类更重要"，然后所有人都会嘲笑你。  

I titled this post "The problem(s) with utilitarianism", but that was a bit of clickbait — people have been pointing out the problems with utilitarianism for over two centuries.  
我将本文命名为《功利主义的问题》有点标题党——两个多世纪来人们一直在指出这些问题。  

One very obvious problem is the distribution of utility. The principle of "the greatest good for the greatest number" doesn’t take a stand on who gets to be happy and who has to be sad.  
最明显的问题是效用分配问题。"最大多数人的最大幸福"原则并未规定谁该幸福、谁该痛苦。  

Would it be good to have a society where one very greedy person gets more and more of what he wants, but never comes any closer to being satisfied, while everyone else is kept on the edge of starvation? This is the utility monster.  
如果一个贪婪者不断获得更多所欲之物却永不满足，而其他人都在饥饿边缘挣扎，这样的社会好吗？这就是"效用怪物"悖论。  

What about a society where one person gets perpetually tortured but millions live in blissful utopia? This is "The Ones Who Walk Away From Omelas."  
如果一个人永远受折磨，而数百万人生活在幸福乌托邦呢？这就是《离开欧米拉斯的人》的命题。  

It’s not hard to come up with mental examples that satisfy the formal principles of utility maximization but which obviously seem grossly unfair.  
我们很容易构想出符合效用最大化形式原则，却明显极不公平的思想实验。  

Another problem is that the set of people whose utility you’re maximizing isn’t fixed. Is it better to have a very large population living on the edge of...  
另一个问题是效用最大化的对象群体并不固定。让大量人口生活在...边缘更好吗？
