Where are the similarities and differences between a data analyst and a quant? If you want to move from DA to quant, how should you go about it? This might be a relatively broad question. Generally speaking, quants are in the finance field; they use mathematical modeling and programming methods to solve financial or investment problems. The most traditional example is what people might call pricing roles in investment banks—you know, pricing. For instance, if you've seen the movie The Big Short, there are people who need to do very complex mathematical modeling and pricing models for derivatives. Many students may have studied financial mathematics or financial engineering, so you've certainly learned things like stochastic processes. It's about using these mathematical methods to assist in the pricing of financial products. These are typically the sell-side pricing quants in investment banks. Quants that lean more toward the buy side, on the other hand, might focus more on investment issues. For example, I need to find signals in the market among countless market data points and then execute investment decisions. There are also quants that assist with risk management. Because whether you're on the buy side or the sell side, whether you're a trader or a portfolio manager, you'll have your own asset portfolio, and you need to measure the risk of that portfolio. This part also requires quants to help with risk assessment. Okay, now DA is data analyst. The main difference is that it is not limited to the financial industry, right? Every industry has data to analyze, whether it's the internet industry, automotive industry, AI industry, consulting industry, and so on. The data they analyze may differ. These data analysts mainly work within their own industry, using data analysis to support business decisions. However, generally speaking, the level of mathematical modeling and programming difficulty is somewhat lower than that for quants. Okay, so if I want to switch from DA to quant, how should I think about this transition path? Personally, I feel that for any cross-career transition, a good approach is to find the intersection between the two roles. For example, if you're doing data analysis in the healthcare field, then you're probably not closely related to finance, right? The data you look at every day is completely different from what derivatives traders look at. So what do you do then? A faster approach might be to find a direction like the health industry and work as a quant or data analyst there. For instance, you could assist analysts in this industry with finding signals or doing more in-depth analysis, because from your experience and skills, this is where the overlap is greatest. Or, for example, some students may be in insurance; you could assist insurance companies with investment strategy analysis, or do something like insurance consulting. After all, your knowledge and skills in the insurance industry are still there. In short, this approach of finding intersections might be a relatively quick entry point for a cross-industry career change.
