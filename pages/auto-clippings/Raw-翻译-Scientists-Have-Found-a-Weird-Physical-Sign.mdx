【文章标题】：Scientists Have Found a Weird Physical Sign That Someone Is Interested in You

【文章标题】：科学家发现了一个对方对你有兴趣的奇特身体信号

【文章正文】：

Along with all the other nightmares that come with dating, the question of whether the person you are doing it with actually likes you is certainly up there in terms of sources of stress.

约会中除了各种令人头疼的问题外，"对方是否真的喜欢你"这个疑问无疑是最大的压力来源之一。

In one study which set people speed dating and monitored the results, researchers found an unusual sign to look out for; how they are swaying their bodies.

在一项组织快速约会并监测结果的研究中，研究人员发现了一个值得关注的异常信号：人们身体的摆动方式。

You don't need IFLScience to tell you that throughout life, we give away a lot of unconscious signals through our body language.

无需科学杂志提醒，我们一生中都会通过肢体语言传递大量无意识的信号。

Smiling all the way to the eyes, for example, can show familiarity, whilst clutching your stomach and rubbing it might indicate you are in severe need of a poop, if my own children are anything to go by.

比如眼带笑意可能表示亲近，而捂着肚子揉搓（以我家孩子为例）可能说明你急需上厕所。

As well as this, how well your own body movements sync up with the people around you can tell onlookers a few things about your relationship.

此外，你的身体动作与周围人的同步程度，也能让旁观者窥见你们的关系状态。

Researchers from McMaster's LIVELab were initially interested in looking into "interpersonal synchronization" as people's movements start to sync up when performing a simple task.

麦克马斯特大学LIVELab的研究人员最初对"人际同步"现象产生兴趣，因为人们在完成简单任务时动作会趋于同步。

But with the equipment at hand already, in 2018 the team turned their attention to speed dating.

2018年，研究团队利用现有设备将注意力转向了快速约会。

"We don’t usually look at romantic relationships," lead author Andrew Chang explained in a statement at the time.

"我们通常不研究恋爱关系，"首席作者Andrew Chang在当时声明中解释。

"What we were interested in at the beginning was how people work together to do something simple, like move an object. From a previous study, we developed a tool that can measure how individuals cooperate with each other based on their body language – more specifically, their body sway."

"我们最初感兴趣的是人们如何协作完成简单任务，比如移动物体。通过前期研究，我们开发出能根据肢体语言（具体说是身体摆动）测量个体协作程度的工具。"

Chang and the team attempted to find out whether it was possible to tell whether potential romantic partners were interested in each other by analyzing their body sway, and how well their bodies swayed in sync with each other.

Chang团队试图通过分析身体摆动及同步程度，判断潜在情侣是否对彼此有兴趣。

"When people coordinate joint actions, have a conversation, have a psychotherapy session or play music in an ensemble, their body sways tend to be coupled," the team explained in their paper.

研究论文指出："当人们协调联合行动、交谈、进行心理治疗或合奏音乐时，身体摆动往往会产生耦合。"

"Although hand gestures and other types of movement can play a role in interpersonal interactions, body sway is a global measurement of an individual’s actions that continues over time; furthermore, it is less affected by task-specific movement requirements than, for example, hand or arm gestures."

"尽管手势和其他动作在人际互动中起作用，但身体摆动是对个体行为的全局性持续测量，且比手势等动作更少受特定任务要求影响。"

The team set their speed daters up with motion capture markers to keep track of their movements, whilst setting them on their four minute dates, which were also filmed with infrared cameras.

研究人员为快速约会者安装动作捕捉标记，用红外摄像机记录他们4分钟约会中的动作。

As well as this, the participants were asked to fill in various questionnaires asking them whether they were looking for a long-term relationship, and whether they wished to see the other participants for a second date.

参与者还需填写问卷，说明是否寻求长期关系及是否愿意二次约会。

All that was left to do (ok, apart from a few other tidbits) was to control for how hot the participants were; attempting to control for factors like one person being unreasonably attractive, and skewing the results.

最后（除其他细节外）需要控制参与者的外貌吸引力，避免因某人过分迷人而扭曲结果。

"We found that directional body sway coupling predicted interest in long-term, but not short-term relationships," the team wrote.

团队写道："我们发现定向身体摆动耦合能预测长期关系意向，而非短期关系。"

"Specifically, participants had higher interest in a long-term relationship if their body sway dynamics better predicted their partner’s body sway, even when controlling for perceived physical attractiveness."

"具体而言，当参与者身体摆动能更好预测伴侣摆动时，即使排除外貌吸引力因素，他们对长期关系的兴趣仍更高。"

The team was intrigued that the effect only predicted interest in long-term relationships.

该效应仅对长期关系有效令团队颇感兴趣。

"We propose that directional body sway coupling reflects the quality of inter-partner communication, which reveals the degree of compatible personality characteristics," they added.

他们补充："我们认为定向身体摆动耦合反映了伴侣间沟通质量，能揭示性格特征的兼容程度。"

"This is consistent with our music ensemble studies, in which directional body sway coupling reflected directional interpersonal communication and predicted the quality of ensemble musical performances."

"这与我们乐队研究一致——定向身体摆动耦合反映定向人际沟通，并能预测合奏质量。"

The team also explored whether playing different types of music could encourage this coupled body sway, and affect further romantic interest in the participants.

团队还探究了不同音乐类型是否能促进身体摆动耦合，进而影响浪漫意向。

At half the speed dating events, participants listened to mellow music including Pink Floyd's Comfortably Numb, whilst the other half listened to "high-groove" including Beyonce’s "Nasty Girl," Stevie Wonder’s "Superstition" and LaBelle’s version of "Lady Marmalade".

半数约会播放平克·弗洛伊德《舒适的麻木》等舒缓音乐，另一半播放碧昂斯《坏女孩》、史提夫·汪达《迷信》等动感音乐。

Whilst the difference was slight, it was still there.

虽然差异微小，但确实存在。

"The percentage of people of people who listened to the low-groove music who wanted to date again was 45 per cent, while the percentage of high-groove listeners who wanted a second date was 50 per cent," Chang added.

Chang补充："听舒缓音乐者二次约会意愿为45%，而动感音乐听众达50%。"

"It’s not the strongest factor in the world, but it indicates that music that encourages coordinated movement can lead to a greater feeling of connection and a greater desire for a second date."

"这虽非决定性因素，但表明促进协调动作的音乐能增强联结感和二次约会欲望。"

The study is published in Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience.

该研究发表于《社会认知与情感神经科学》期刊。
