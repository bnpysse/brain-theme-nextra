Where Germany’s 17 Million Immigrants Were Born

Key Takeaways:

About 17 million people living in Germany were born abroad, compared with 4.3 million German-born people living overseas.

Poland is the largest birthplace among Germany’s foreign-born population, while the U.S. is the leading destination for German-born emigrants.

Germany has expanded skilled-worker immigration pathways to help address labor shortages.

Germany is one of the world’s largest destinations for international migrants, shaped by decades of labor migration, European mobility, and more recent refugee arrivals.

Poland accounts for 11% of Germany’s foreign-born population, followed by Türkiye at 9% and Ukraine at 8.5%. These populations reflect several

migration waves

, including postwar labor recruitment, EU labor mobility, and more recent humanitarian migration from Ukraine and Syria.

Germany’s broader migration footprint extends beyond its foreign-born population. In 2025, 21.8 million residents, or 26.3% of the population, had an “immigration history,” meaning they had immigrated themselves or both parents had immigrated. This measure also includes some people born in Germany, making it broader than the foreign-born population shown in the graphic.

Germany’s Immigration Policies

Labor shortages have helped shape Germany’s immigration policies. The expanded

Skilled Immigration Act

broadened pathways for qualified workers, including an Opportunity Card for job seekers and expanded access to the EU Blue Card. According to the OECD, reforms implemented in 2024 also made the recognition of foreign qualifications more flexible.

Alongside these labor migration measures, Germany has pursued integration programs and policies addressing asylum and irregular migration.

Where German-Born Emigrants Live

The

U.S. hosts

the largest population of German-born emigrants, at 1.1 million, or roughly one in four living abroad.

European countries also feature prominently. Switzerland, the UK, and Austria are among the largest destinations, reflecting geographic proximity, employment opportunities, and established migration links. Türkiye also ranks highly, illustrating how migration between the two countries extends in both directions.
