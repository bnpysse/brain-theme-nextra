【文章标题】：Nobody Asked for a Crab Chair
《没人想要螃蟹椅》

【文章正文】：
In our last installment of "Good Intentions Gone Wrong: When Disability Engineers Go Bad," we highlighted a particularly egregious Swiss wheelchair design featuring superfluous drones. 
在我们上期《善意变坏事：当残障工程师跑偏时》专栏中，曾揭露过一款配备多余无人机的瑞士轮椅设计。

Thankfully, not all engineers who receive blank checks to design disability solutions are that out of touch. 
值得庆幸的是，并非所有获得空白支票设计残障解决方案的工程师都如此脱离实际。

More commonly, the lack of constraints that comes with deep corporate pockets results in sexy, future-forward designs with tantalizing possibilities that somehow leave me feeling more empty than excited.
更常见的情况是，资金雄厚的企业因缺乏约束，最终产出那些充满未来感、可能性诱人却让我倍感空虚的炫酷设计。

Toyota’s new-ish "Walk me" prototype could be the poster chair for what I’m talking about. 
丰田新近推出的"Walk me"原型机堪称这类设计的典范。

The "Walk me" replaces wheels with four articulating legs that bend and move independently. 
这款产品用四条可独立弯曲移动的机械腿取代了传统轮椅。

Picture a four-legged crab with light-up legs carefully balancing an uncomfortable-looking seat.
想象一只四脚发光螃蟹，正小心翼翼驮着看起来就不舒服的座椅。

Using LiDAR, collision radars, and internal weight sensors, the crabchair can transport its user over uneven terrain or up and down stairs. 
借助激光雷达、碰撞雷达和内置重量传感器，这把螃蟹椅能载人跨越崎岖地形或上下楼梯。

With an aesthetically-pleasing pastel color palette and a chic, future-minimalist vibe, it’s easy to see why the crabchair was a hit when Toyota unveiled it last fall at the Japan Mobility Show.
凭借悦目的马卡龙配色和时尚的极简未来风，难怪去年秋季在日本移动展亮相时就引发轰动。

Let’s be clear: if I could have a free crabchair tomorrow, I’d be out the door, in the line to pick up my new robot legs before I knew where to go.
说实在的：如果明天能免费领到螃蟹椅，我绝对会冲出门排队——哪怕根本不知道去哪领。

But these kinds of prototypes are built to inspire and show what is possible, not to be mass produced for actual users. 
但这类原型本就是为了展示可能性，而非真正量产。

In short, don’t get your hopes up you’ll ever see a crabchair clawing its way down the street.
简言之，别指望哪天能看到螃蟹椅横行街头。

They’re over-designed, cost prohibitive and generally out of touch with the realities facing most would-be users. 
它们过度设计、造价惊人，与多数潜在用户的实际需求严重脱节。

They’re engineering inspiration porn. 
堪称工程界的励志毒鸡汤。

They make headlines (yes, I’m guilty), and make people feel like they’re helping an underserved community and making the world a better place. 
虽然能上头条（我也有责任），让人误以为帮助了弱势群体，美化了世界。

Unfortunately, they very rarely translate into real world benefits for people like us.
可惜对我们而言，这些设计鲜少带来实际益处。

That alone would be disappointing, but probably closer to a "no harm, no foul" situation: rich people and companies building expensive toys and patting themselves on the back. 
若仅止于此，顶多算是"无害无咎"：富豪企业烧钱造玩具自我感动罢了。

It has been going on forever and likely will into the future.
这类事自古有之，未来也不会绝迹。

My concern, and the thing that saps any enthusiasm the sleek design might inspire in me, is that much like more traditional inspiration porn harms the disabled community by propagating false narratives and not doing justice to real experiences of disabled people, this engineering version of inspiration porn neglects to address the design problems most critical to our community while pulling away obviously-talented designers and chair-loads of cash that could have been focused on solving them.
真正令我担忧的是——正如传统励志毒鸡汤通过传播虚假叙事、歪曲残障者真实处境造成伤害——这种工程版毒鸡汤不仅转移了本可用于解决关键问题的天才设计师和巨额资金，更忽视了残障群体最迫切的设计需求，这彻底浇灭了我对炫酷设计的热情。

I would give at least a few of my quad fingers for a high quality, lightweight, low-profile indoor/outdoor power chair with the customizable seating and back options I rely on. 
我宁愿用几根手指换取一款高质量、轻量化、低矮型室内外两用电动轮椅，带可定制座椅和靠背。

Same for a stable shower chair that could safely navigate step-in showers. 
或是能安全进出步入式淋浴间的稳固浴椅。

I’m not talking about reinventing the wheel(chair), but tweaking and refining the designs we have to optimize independence and function for users.
我并非要求颠覆性创新，而是希望优化现有设计来提升用户自主性和功能性。

Like I wrote in the previous article in this series, there are brilliant designers working on these and other similar issues but we need more! 
正如本系列前文所述，虽有不少设计师在攻克这些问题，但我们仍需更多力量！

More minds, more money, more eyeballs focused on improving what we have and doing so in affordable ways.
更多人才、资金和关注，来以可负担的方式改进现有产品。

I genuinely appreciate the designers behind the "Walk me" (even if I wholeheartedly question whomever decided the name should sound like wheelchair users are the crabchair’s pets). 
我由衷感谢"Walk me"的设计师（尽管命名者把轮椅用户比作螃蟹椅宠物的脑回路令我困惑）。

I hope their experience designing the crabchair got them excited about disability-related design and that they continue to pursue it. 
希望这次设计经历能点燃他们对残障相关设计的热情。

All I ask is that instead of sitting in their corporate labs, bouncing ideas off each other, they get out into the real world. 
只愿他们能走出企业实验室，深入现实世界。

They should dedicate themselves to engaging and listening to the disability community about what problems need solving and what does and doesn’t work. 
认真倾听残障群体对真实需求与使用痛点的反馈。

We know. We get it.
我们最清楚实际情况。

Lord knows we don’t need any more designs like this…
天知道我们不需要更多这类设计...
