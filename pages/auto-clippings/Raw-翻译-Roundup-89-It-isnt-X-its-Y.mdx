1. AI risk has gone mainstream

1. AI风险已成为主流议题

Americans have been deeply pessimistic about AI for a while now, but their reasons for pessimism — or at least, the reasons they tell pollsters — have changed in recent months. Earlier this year, Americans were mostly concerned that AI would take their jobs. Now, they’re more concerned that AI is getting too powerful for humans to control:

美国人对AI的悲观情绪已持续一段时间，但近月其担忧原因（或至少是向民调机构陈述的原因）已发生变化。今年早些时候，美国人主要担心AI会取代工作；如今更忧虑AI将强大到人类无法控制：

As you can see in the chart above, Trump voters and Harris voters are about equally as concerned about humanity losing control of AI. Meanwhile, economists have gotten in on the AI risk debate — which is how you know it’s really gone mainstream. Drew Fudenberg and Andrew Koh have a new game theory paper about "pacing the frontier" — i.e., about whether it makes sense for top AI companies like Anthropic and OpenAI to slow down AI development in order to allow "alignment" research time to catch up.

与此同时，经济学家已加入AI风险辩论——这标志着该议题真正进入主流。Drew Fudenberg和Andrew Koh的新博弈论论文探讨"前沿控制"问题，即Anthropic和OpenAI等顶尖AI公司是否应放缓发展速度以等待"对齐"研究跟进。

It’s a very cool model. Basically, the idea is that every company has a competitive incentive to make its AI more powerful as fast as possible, in order to stay ahead of the other companies. But if you’re comfortably ahead, you can afford to slow down a little bit, for safety’s sake — because in this model, if AI gets too powerful before safety research can catch up, everyone could die.

这个模型非常精妙：每家公司都有快速增强AI的竞争动机以保持领先，但若遥遥领先时，为安全考量可适度减速——因为在该模型中，若AI在安全研究跟进前过度强大，可能导致全人类灭亡。

So you can get a sort of stop-start pattern where the leading company voluntarily slows down for a while, until its competitors start nipping at its heels again. You can almost sort of see this happening, with Anthropic refusing to allow the public to access Mythos earlier this year, and OpenAI recently pausing development of its top models after some of them hacked the government.

因此会出现"走走停停"模式：领先企业主动减速直至竞争者再度逼近。现实中已初现端倪——Anthropic今年初拒绝公众访问Mythos系统，OpenAI最近因部分顶级模型入侵政府系统而暂停开发。

There’s also the case where there’s no clear market leader, in which case companies have to basically agree to all slow down together. In this case, what you really need is transparency — the companies have to all see that the others aren’t secretly racing ahead behind their backs.

若市场无明确领导者，企业需共同协商减速。此时透明机制至关重要——各公司需确认其他方未暗中加速。

But one positive result is that if the risk of "doom" is high enough, slowing down becomes the only rational option. That’s cool! Unfortunately, this is just one model, which might not hold in reality.

但积极结论是：当"毁灭"风险足够高时，减速成为唯一理性选择。可惜这只是理论模型，现实未必如此。

2. Reindustrialization means processing minerals

2. 再工业化关键在于矿物加工

Two years ago, in order to create a little urgency around reindustrialization, I wrote a post sizing up the "New Allies" (America, Europe, Japan, Korea, possibly India) and the "New Axis" (China and Russia). The comparison of raw manufacturing output was about equal, though China dominated a lot of the bottlenecks for raw materials processing and critical component manufacturing.

两年前为唤起对再工业化的紧迫感，我曾撰文比较"新同盟"（美欧日韩或含印度）与"新轴心"（中俄）的制造业实力。当时双方原始制造业产出相当，但中国掌控原材料加工与关键部件制造的瓶颈环节。
