What if the best way for an AI to

remember a book is to stop reading it?

You see, every word an AI reads becomes

tokens, and tokens are expensive. The

longer the book, the more the AI chokes

on it. But, we humans do something

strange. We don't remember everything

perfectly. Some details fade. The older

grandpa is, the better he was at school.

And scientists at Deep Seek asked the

question, "What if we make an AI do

that, too?" Asking artificial

intelligence to do artificial

forgetting.

如果让AI记住一本书的最佳方式是停止阅读它呢？你看，AI读到的每一个词都会变成令牌（tokens），而令牌是昂贵的。书越长，AI就越难以处理。但我们人类会做一些奇怪的事情——我们不会完美地记住一切，有些细节会逐渐模糊。爷爷年纪越大，他在学校的表现就越好（这句是调侃记忆随年龄衰退）。Deep Seek的科学家们提出了一个问题：“如果我们让AI也这样做呢？”——让人工智能进行人工遗忘。

But, what a crazy proposition. Why would

you do that? How does that even make

sense? Well, let's see together. As we

keep talking to an AI system, the longer

we talk, the more documents we attach,

the more expensive it is to keep running

because it has to remember everything.

Now, Deep Seek OCR says not to worry.

Let's take this big soup of information,

even text, and render it as an image,

and then compress that image, a bit like

what JPEG compression does to images.

But, this is learned and token-based.

And now, this data is drastically

smaller. But, this still doesn't make

too much sense. So, how is that good? I

mean, we lose a lot of information,

right? Well, hold on to your papers,

fellow scholars, and let's see how much

information we lose at 10x compression

level that is throwing away almost

everything and using 90% less space.

That is a lot of compression. And what

can that be? We can recover more than

96% of the text. That is insane. So,

why not push it a bit further? Well, as

we go up to nearly 20x compression, OCR

accuracy falls to 60%.

但这是一个多么疯狂的主张。为什么要这样做？这怎么可能说得通？好吧，让我们一起看看。当我们持续与AI系统对话时，对话越长，附加的文档越多，运行成本就越高，因为它必须记住所有内容。现在，Deep Seek OCR说别担心。让我们把这锅信息浓汤——即使是文本——渲染成图像，然后压缩该图像，有点像JPEG压缩对图像所做的那样。但这是经过学习且基于令牌的。现在，这些数据大幅缩小了。但这仍然不太说得通。那么，这有什么好处呢？我的意思是，我们会丢失大量信息，对吧？好吧，各位学者，请抓紧你们的论文，让我们看看在10倍压缩级别下我们会丢失多少信息——这几乎是丢弃一切，节省90%的空间。这是很大的压缩量。结果会怎样呢？我们可以恢复超过96%的文本。这太不可思议了。那么，为什么不更进一步呢？当我们接近20倍压缩时，OCR准确率下降到60%。

Wow. I expected much, much lower. Okay,

now important question. Is this really

new? I mean, at first I was a bit

confused by this work because this is an

unassuming paper about OCR. Yes, this

does OCR. So, you give it a document,

maybe even a handwritten note, and it

gives you the text back. Formally, it

takes a small amount of compressed

vision tokens and turns them into a much

larger number of text tokens. But, is

this just JPEG? I mean, that technology

is more than 30 years old. The idea is

good, but is this just JPEG plugged into

an AI system? Well, not quite. There is

tons of new stuff here. Dear fellow

scholars, this is Two Minute Papers with

Dr. Karolis Jonaityte. You see, JPEG

compresses pixels. This compresses

memories. First, these memories are

turned into a picture, and then we

compress that picture. But, we are not

done yet. No, no, no.

Not even close. The key engineering

trick is that they created an encoder

that is built to survive very

high-resolution documents without

exploding in cost. To do that, one, it

uses local window attention first. Two,

a 16x convolutional compressor. And

three, it does global visual processing.

Okay, and what does that mean? Well, in

other words, it does not stare at every

pixel with full attention. No, it skims,

it squeezes, and only then it reads. And

here is something brilliant. Once you

can do this cheaply enough, suddenly

this is not just about remembering old

data. Wait, does that mean that it can

not only understand documents, but also

help generate new data? Yes. The paper

says it can generate training data for

LLMs and VLMs at more than 200,000

pages per day on a single A100 GPU in

sane speeds. Now, we are fellow scholars

here, so we look at limitations, too.

The paper itself says OCR alone is not

enough to prove true text compression,

and a later stress test suggests the

model may rely heavily on language

priors, not just visual memory. In other

words, when the image becomes too

compressed, it may not truly see the

missing text anymore, but it also

guesses the most likely words from the

context, kind of like auto complete, but

better.

哇，我原本预期会低得多得多。好了，现在有个重要问题：这真的是新东西吗？我的意思是，起初我对这项工作有点困惑，因为这是一篇不起眼的关于OCR的论文。是的，它确实做OCR。你给它一份文档，甚至可能是手写笔记，它会把文本还给你。形式上，它取少量压缩的视觉令牌，并将它们转换成数量大得多的文本令牌。但这只是JPEG吗？我的意思是，那项技术已经30多年了。这个想法很好，但这只是把JPEG接入AI系统吗？嗯，不完全是。这里有很多新东西。亲爱的各位学者，这里是“两分钟论文”，我是Karolis Jonaityte博士。你看，JPEG压缩像素，而这压缩记忆。首先，这些记忆被转换成图片，然后我们压缩那张图片。但我们还没完。不不不，远未结束。关键的工程技巧在于，他们创建了一个编码器，其设计目的是在不导致成本爆炸的情况下处理超高分辨率文档。为此，第一，它首先使用局部窗口注意力；第二，使用16倍卷积压缩器；第三，进行全局视觉处理。好了，这意味着什么？换句话说，它不会用全部注意力盯着每个像素。不，它先略读、挤压，然后才读取。这里有个绝妙之处：一旦你能足够廉价地做到这一点，突然之间，这不仅仅是关于记住旧数据了。等等，这是否意味着它不仅能理解文档，还能帮助生成新数据？是的。论文称，它可以在单个A100 GPU上以合理的速度每天为LLM和VLM生成超过20万页的训练数据。既然我们是同行学者，我们也要看看局限性。论文本身指出，仅靠OCR不足以证明真正的文本压缩，后续的压力测试表明，该模型可能严重依赖语言先验，而不仅仅是视觉记忆。换句话说，当图像被过度压缩时，它可能不再真正“看到”缺失的文本，而是根据上下文猜测最可能的词语，有点像自动补全，但更好。
